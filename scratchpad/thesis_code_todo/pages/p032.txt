featurization, nodes encode atom type, chirality, hybridization and aromaticity, and
edges encode bond type, ring membership, direction and stereochemistry [35].
   In the standard molecular graph, atoms are mapped to nodes and bonds to edges
[6]. These molecular graphs are typically 2D objects; 3D information such as atomic
coordinates and bond angles is not part of the graph unless added as node or edge
attributes [6]. However, there are alternative molecular graph representations such
as graphs with quantum 3D embeddings, weight alignment graphs, association graphs
containing pairs of molecules, and geometric interaction graph neural networks that
incorporate 3D structures and physical interactions [36–38]. Du et al. [39] developed
hybrid 2D and 3D graphs, combining 3D data, guaranteeing precision and complete-
ness by providing unabridged information, and 2D data, providing chemical intuitions
for guidance, in a zipper-like arrangement. Voxel-based 3D representations, in which
each atom occupies one grid cell, are too sparse for convolutional networks to perform
well; Kuzminykh et al. [40] instead spread each atom over nearby voxels with a wave
transform.
   These graph representations do not highlight specific substructures or specific ar-
rangements of atoms and bonds within each molecule. The substructures contain
functional groups that are related to a range of chemical properties and are poten-
tially involved in downstream tasks related to the function of molecules. Bouritsas
et al. [41] developed graph substructure networks, a topologically aware message pass-
ing scheme based on substructure encoding. The method involves encoding structural
roles based on the appearance of specific substructures and using them in message
passing updates. Since message-passing graph neural networks (GNNs) are at most as
powerful as the Weisfeiler–Lehman test, they cannot isolate sufficient features from the
graph structure [41]. Graph substructure networks, on the other hand, capture these
substructure-related features. Encoders pretrained on unlabeled molecules, whether
graph-based [9] or transformers on SMILES [42], can also produce embeddings that
capture structural features.

Selected by a detector

The main cleanup experiment takes the out-of-fold forest residual as its detector and
crosses it with each action above. The cliff-aware gap is graded as a detector beside it.
A second experiment asks whether to delete a flagged molecule or keep it, and keeps it
either with a lower weight or with a moved label. Its control deletes the same number of
molecules at random, at five seeds. A third experiment replaces the forest residual with
each of five detectors in turn: the cliff-aware gap, and the GP, the heteroscedastic GP,
the variance-head network and NGBoost. Each deletes its top 5%, 10% or 20%. Where
the detector has its own prediction, it also moves every label toward that prediction.
The share moved is given by Equation 7.2, or by ci = αsi /(1 + αsi ), which uses the
score in its own units.
   Four experiments weight molecules by a neighbor or model detector, with no dele-
tion. Three use the weight 1/(1 + 3s̃i ), under which the most suspect molecule counts
a quarter as much as the least suspect. They differ in where the neighbors are found:
in features scaled by importance on clean labels, in the importance-scaled space above,
and in each of the four families of distance. The fourth uses exp(−0.1 s̃i ) with the gap
over neighbor spread, the GP or NGBoost as the detector. With that weight, the most
suspect molecule counts about 0.9 times as much as the least suspect.
   Close-pair weights look only at pairs of near-identical molecules. A pair is close
if its distance is in the lowest 1% of all training pairs. The label noise is estimated
from the closest fifth of those pairs as σ̂ 2 = ∆y 2 /2, since two noisy copies of one value
differ by twice the noise variance. Both molecules of any close pair whose labels differ

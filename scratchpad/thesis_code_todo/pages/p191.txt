[261, 262]. Another is the cleanlab regression score [263], which draws its internal folds
at random rather than by scaffold family. The rest divide the residual by a spread for
that molecule, from NGBoost, a quantile forest or a conformal interval.
       Two experiments test the out-of-fold LightGBM residual under the shift in 10% sized
by model error. The first ranks molecules by the residual itself. The second divides
each residual by a second LightGBM model’s out-of-fold estimate of how large that
molecule’s residual usually is. It then turns the result into conformal p-values against
a calibration set of training molecules [264]. Molecules are flagged with the Benjamini–
Hochberg rule [265], which bounds the expected share of false flags at 5%, 10% or 20%.
The calibration set is a random 20% or 50% of the training molecules or, where a
dataset has repeat measurements, the molecules with three or more measurements
that agree. Both experiments grade the ranking by the area under the ROC curve.
       A detector can be graded only where the damage moved some labels and not others,
so that it is known which molecules were damaged. That is outlier noise, censoring,
the two shifts in 10% and every label flip. Under Gaussian and grouped-shifted noise
every label moves, and no detection score exists. We grade a detector by the average
precision of its suspicion scores against the set of damaged molecules. A detector that
ranks at random scores the damaged share of the training molecules; that share is the
chance level. Some experiments report the area under the ROC curve instead, where
chance is 0.5.

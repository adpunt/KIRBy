   This chapter addresses the questions set out in Chapter 1, specifically how the choice
of representation and model architecture explain the variance in predictive performance
and in noise robustness, what is the impact of the type of label noise, and whether
uncertainty estimates track label noise.


3.2     Methods
We looked at six representations paired with thirteen models, and several variant mod-
els, across four prediction tasks. Every pairing was run under Gaussian, grouped-wider
and grouped-shifted noise at seven noise levels, and the other four noise conditions
(Section 2.5.1) were run on selected pairings. The hyperparameters of every model are
given in Additional file 2.


3.2.1     Datasets
Although we are investigating the effects of label noise stemming from experimental
settings, for the purposes of testing we inject controlled amounts of artificial noise into
clean labels. Data were extracted from the QM9 molecular property dataset distributed
by PyTorch Geometric [109], which contains 130,831 small organic molecules with
pre-computed quantum-mechanical properties obtained from density functional theory
(DFT) calculations at the B3LYP/6-31G(2df,p) level of theory. We selected the gap
between the highest occupied and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (HOMO–
LUMO gap) as the prediction target in QM9. We assumed that QM9 labels were
free of noise, notwithstanding approximations in the level of theory. Each experiment
was replicated 10 times with a distinct random seed and a randomly selected subset
of N = 10,000 molecules, split 80/10/10 into 8,000 training, 1,000 validation and
1,000 test molecules. All models were fitted on those 8,000 training molecules, except
the Gaussian processes, which were fitted on a random subset of 5,000 of them; the
validation and test molecules were unchanged.

mentally determined endpoints. From the OpenADMET initiative [110], we used LogD
and Caco-2 efflux permeability, with N = 5,039 and N = 2,161 respectively. We also
selected hERG Ki data from ChEMBL [111], following a protocol inspired by Landrum
and Riniker [16]: filter for binding assays, deduplicate by median pChEMBL value, and

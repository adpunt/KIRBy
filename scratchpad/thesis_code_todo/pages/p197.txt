least squares on their out-of-fold predictions [280]. The damage is Gaussian noise, and
Gaussian noise whose variance is a multiple, 0.1, 0.3 or 1.0, of a LightGBM model’s
out-of-fold mean squared error on the clean labels. This experiment ran on Caco-2,
hERG Ki and LogD.


The expanded published sweep

The twelve published methods of Chapter 4 are run again (Table 7.5). Every one uses
the same network as the plain model, with three hidden layers of 256, 128 and 64 units
and dropout 0.2. It is trained with the Adam optimizer at a learning rate of 10−3 for
up to 200 epochs, with early stopping. The code is a new implementation of the same
twelve, not a rerun of the earlier study. It is wider in four ways: it runs on many
datasets instead of QM9 alone, under every damage kind instead of Gaussian noise
alone, and on hybrids as well as single representations. It also scores each method
against the plain network on the same damaged labels in the same feature space. Four
of the twelve select molecules with a detector and delete them, so under Table 2.3
they are noise mitigation, and the table marks them so. Most were built for image
classification, and several of our versions differ from their source, as the table states.


Robust losses

The network is also trained with a Huber loss [98], whose threshold is c times 1.4826
times the median absolute out-of-fold residual, with c of 0.5, 1, 1.5 or 2. Under the shift
in 10% sized by model error, each model’s own robust loss is tried at c = 1.5. That is
a Huber loss for LightGBM and the network, a pseudo-Huber loss for XGBoost, and
absolute-error splits for the random forest.


Two learners

Two learners, LightGBM and an SVM, each score every molecule by its out-of-fold
error. For two rounds, each is refitted on the molecules the other finds easiest. Each
then keeps its own easiest 80%, 90% or 95%, and the downstream model is fitted on
the molecules both keep. This follows the exchange of co-teaching [151], done between
whole refits rather than inside one training loop. Its control deletes the same number
of molecules at random, at three seeds.

Distance against prediction error

This follows Sheridan et al. [251], who found that a molecule’s similarity to the training
molecules tracks how well it is predicted. Each downstream model is fitted twice, on
all representations joined and on its own first hybrid, and we record its error on every
held-out molecule. On regression data this is the absolute error, and on classification
data it is one minus the predicted probability of the true class. We then compute
the Spearman correlation between that error and the molecule’s mean distance to its
nearest training molecules, at 5, 10 and 20 neighbors and every distance. We do this
in each feature space of Section 6.2.1, unweighted and weighted.


Retrieval and clustering

Retrieval measures how close a molecule’s neighbors are to it in label. For each held-out
molecule, the label gap (lower is better) is the weighted mean absolute difference be-
tween its label and those of its nearest training molecules, at every setting of Table 6.1.
On classification data, it is one minus the weighted share of neighbors in the molecule’s
class. For clustering, we fit scikit-learn’s k-means [252] on the training molecules with
3, 5 and 10 clusters, and assign each held-out molecule to a cluster. Label coherence
(higher is better) is one minus the mean standard deviation of the held-out labels within
a cluster, divided by the standard deviation of all held-out labels. On classification
data, it is the mean share of the largest class in each cluster. Clusters with fewer than
two held-out molecules are left out.

   The per-molecule lift tests whether the size of each lift matters. On LogD, the
network on the hybrid scores 0.499 RMSE with each molecule’s own lift, against 0.463
for the best of three shuffles. On LogD and Caco-2 every shuffle beats the molecule’s

(Table 7.21). On QM9 the first hybrid’s own lift beats one shuffle of three and loses to
the other two. What matters is knowing which labels are clipped and raising them by
about the right average amount, which is what the constant lift does.
   Censoring did not need fixing on two antibody sets. On SMAC colloidal stability
and ∆∆G, clipping a quarter of the training labels raised the held-out Spearman in
most configurations (Additional file 41). On ∆∆G, the support vector regressor on
the first single representation goes 0.119 before clipping and 0.248 after. With nothing
lost, censored EM had nothing to recover, and on both datasets it moved the score
back down in most configurations.
   On QM9 we also clipped a quarter of the training labels on all five outer folds, with
a control: the same move applied to the same number of unclipped labels. Moving the
clipped labels lowered the RMSE with both models in every feature space, and moving
the unclipped labels raised it. With the network on the first hybrid, the RMSE was
0.5072 before treatment and 0.3980 after censored EM.

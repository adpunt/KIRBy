   The aleatoric component indicates that label noise increased, but it doesn’t tell us
by how much (Figure 3.14). For each unit of added noise it rises by roughly 0.4 to 1
unit, depending on model, dataset and representation. The aleatoric component can
therefore compare noise between datasets or noise levels, but it does not estimate a
dataset’s internal error.
   Uncertainty estimation breaks under censoring as the process of clipping the labels
narrows their spread, which in turn shrinks the total uncertainty. None of the models
tested treat a clipped label as a noisy one, so they actually grow more confident while
losing information.

Censored EM repeats this for three rounds, refitting the downstream model out of fold
to the current labels each round, after the iterated estimator of Buckley and James
[275]. The constant lift raises every clipped label to L + σm, the mean of σmi over the
clipped molecules. That shared amount is larger than the truncated-normal mean’s
lift by L − pi on average. Two arms test what the flag is worth. Deleting the clipped
molecules uses the flag but throws the molecules away. A per-molecule lift raises
each clipped label by its own σmi , and its control deals the same lifts to the wrong
clipped molecules, at three seeds. Svensson et al. [83] fitted censored regression to
drug-discovery labels, which is the closest precedent.
       A scaffold family is also a known flag. Under grouped-shifted noise every family
carries its own offset. A family’s mean out-of-fold residual estimates that offset, and
subtracting it from every label in the family undoes it. Three versions differ in how
much they trust a small family. The first subtracts the whole mean residual from every
family, and the second only from families of three or more training molecules. The third
is a random-intercept model, fitted by up to five rounds of expectation maximization
in the style of mixed-effects random forests [276, 277]. It shrinks each family’s offset
toward zero, more for a family with few molecules.

   As on QM9, the noise distribution does not change the predictive capabilities of
a model (Table 3.4). Laplace, Student-t and outlier noise were run on seven models,
RF, the two GPs, SVM, NGBoost, BNN-α (var. head) and VBLL-β (het.), on ECFP4,
PDV and ChemBERTa. Each delta below is measured from the same model’s AUCnorm
under Gaussian noise. RF and the two GPs never lose more than 0.03 of AUCnorm under
these three conditions or under grouped-wider noise, on any dataset or representation.
SVM and NGBoost again move in opposite directions, SVM never loses AUCnorm under
any of the four, while NGBoost loses under all four on Caco-2 and hERG Ki . The two
networks move furthest in both directions. It’s worth remembering that while we are
injecting artificial noise, the assay measurements carry their own source of noise whose
properties are unknown, so no “clean” label is truly clean.
   Of the three noise conditions run on every model, grouped-shifted noise gives the
lowest AUCnorm values on every assay dataset (Figure 3.10). Under it, RF ranks first or
second for AUCnorm with every representation on LogD and with four of six on hERG
Ki . R2 at a noise level of 1.0 depends more on clean performance. By that measure,
SVM ranks first to third on LogD, while RF ranks fifth to ninth across datasets.
   LightGBM has particularly varied results across the datasets. On LogD, it has rel-
atively high predictive performance and middle-of-the-pack noise robustness. However,
on hERG and Caco-2, it loses the most with increased noise, particularly on Gaussian
and grouped-shifted noise. The effects are not as pronounced on grouped-wider, al-
though it still ranks among the least robust there. XGBoost degrades in the same way
on hERG and Caco-2.

5.2.7     Representations by domain
Any representation that gives one fixed-length vector per sample can serve as a base
representation (Section 5.2.1). The first version of the method used three molecular rep-
resentations: PDV, ECFP4 and MHG-GNN. These three are now one small-molecule
example among many. Every base representation is frozen: it is computed once from
the raw input (a SMILES string, a sequence or a reaction) and is never trained on the
labels.
   To choose them, every candidate representation was first scored on its own with a
gradient-boosted tree model [62] under the dataset’s grouped folds. Candidates were
then compared by the Spearman correlation between their out-of-fold predictions, since
two representations whose predictions correlate highly carry much the same signal. At
most ten were then chosen by hand, looking at each candidate’s own score next to
these correlations and favoring representations that encode the input in different ways.
Binding free energy has eight.
   Which representation is best depends on the downstream model as well as the
dataset. The best one is the same under both of a dataset’s downstream models on
three of the seven small-molecule datasets and five of the twelve antibody datasets (Ta-
ble 5.7). On hERG Ki , for example, MHG-GNN is best with the SVM and GROVER
with the DNN. Augmentation features do not count toward the ten, and no search in
this chapter uses them (Section 5.2.3).
   If an input cannot be parsed or embedded, it gets an all-zero row, so that every
representation of a dataset keeps the same rows. In computed descriptors, a missing or
infinite value is set to 0, and values are stored unscaled. A pretrained model gives one
vector per input, the mean of its last-layer token vectors with padding excluded, unless
the tables say otherwise. Scaling and filling of missing values happen inside each fold,
on the training rows only, as described in the construction of the hybrid.


Small molecules

The small-molecule representations (Table 5.9) are the fingerprint, descriptor, graph
and language-model families described in Chapter 2. The only additions are four
sets of 3D shape and pharmacophore features, used as augmentation only. All four
are computed from a single conformer per molecule, embedded with a fixed seed and
optimized with the Merck molecular force field. A single conformer may misdescribe
flexible molecules.

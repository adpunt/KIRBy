piles many labels into one value, which the model learns to tightly predict. Ironically,
per-molecule uncertainty can be used in reverse to identify clipped labels as the labels
a model is more certain about, especially when they fall in a certain range, are most
likely to have been clipped.


3.4      Conclusions
We investigated how a variety of molecular representations and models perform under
increasing label noise. We used ANOVA variance decomposition across three noise
conditions on four datasets to show that molecular representation accounts for a larger
share of the variance in predictive performance than in robustness. Robustness to label
noise, as measured by AUCnorm , the normalized area under the R2 retention curve, is
more impacted by the choice of model. The representation each model is paired with
explains more variance than the representation alone. As such, a representation should
not be chosen independently of the model it will be used with, and some models
benefit from alternative molecular representations across both predictive performance
and noise robustness. Model family was the primary factor in every decomposition
except one, Caco-2 under grouped-shifted noise, where the scaffold fold took the larger
share.

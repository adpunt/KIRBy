Chapter 8

Conclusions and Further Thoughts

This chapter answers the research questions of Chapter 1 on handling label noise in
molecular data, then sets out how to choose a noise-handling method and what the
thesis contributes.


8.1     Summary of findings
The first half of the thesis asked four questions about label noise, answered in Chapter 3.
First, model family decided noise robustness more than the representation did. On
QM9 under Gaussian noise, model family explained 47.2% of the variance in AUCnorm
and the representation far less, whereas the representation mattered more for predictive
performance. Label noise corrupts the labels and leaves the features untouched, so this
split is what one would expect. Second, random forests were the most consistent model
under noise. LightGBM and XGBoost predicted well on clean labels and lost the most
as noise rose, so a model’s score on clean labels says little about how it holds up
under noise. Third, the type of noise mattered more than the shape of its distribution.
Systematic noise, shared within a scaffold family or clipped at an assay limit, did the
most damage. A study that injects only Gaussian noise therefore misses it. Fourth,
mean predicted uncertainty rose with noise, but no model’s uncertainty identified the
corrupted labels.

5.2.6     Datasets and splitting
The hybrid experiments use 20 datasets (Table 5.7): seven of small molecules, 12 of
antibodies and one of reactions. Their curation is given in Additional file S.7 and their
representations in Table 5.12. Antibody datasets outnumber small-molecule ones, but
small molecules are the focus of this chapter, and results are read per dataset, not
pooled across a domain.


Pre-processing

Additional file S.7 gives the shared and per-dataset pre-processing steps, with the
counts after each step. The LogD and Caco-2 labels are clipped at their 1st and 99th
percentiles, so on Caco-2, for example, 22 molecules share the highest label and 23 the
lowest.


Splitting rules

As in Chapter 3 (Section 3.2), no group is split between folds, and classification folds
are also stratified with a fixed seed. Every dataset uses five outer folds, and no split is
random (Section 2.7). On a given dataset, every representation, model and hybrid is
scored on the same folds.


Groups by domain

The small-molecule datasets, QM9 included, are grouped by Bemis–Murcko scaffold
(Section 3.2), here keeping stereochemistry. On QM9 the largest group is the empty
scaffold of acyclic molecules, with 1,041 of the 9,978 molecules. The two datasets from
Biogen [137] (rat microsomal clearance and MDR1–MDCK efflux) have 1.2 and 1.1
molecules per scaffold, respectively, so holding out a scaffold there is close to holding
out single molecules. Chapter 3 used a different scaffold key and different curation for
its versions of LogD, Caco-2, hERG Ki and QM9, and Additional file S.7.2 lists the
differences.

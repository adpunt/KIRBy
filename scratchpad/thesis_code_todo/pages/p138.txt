feature, which keeps all features in the hybrid distinct. Indices and importances are
smaller, and harder to invert, than model weights or gradients. In related work, each
client runs mutual-information feature selection on its own data and a server aggregates
the results [245].
   All four separate-site experiments run on Caco-2 and LogD, with five folds grouped
by scaffold family and three seeds, the DNN and SVM, and budgets of 500 to 2,000
features. In the main one, each site holds the same molecules but one family of rep-
resentations: substructure fingerprints, physicochemical descriptors or pretrained em-
beddings. It compares the best single family, picked on the training molecules, with
one feature selection over all families, and both with one selection per family at an even
share of the budget. When a family is narrower than its share, the rest goes to the
other families. The even split can, as a result, build fewer features than one selection
over all families. The built widths are recorded on every fold.
   Three further experiments, with flipped labels, with one representation per site and
with features selected per cluster, are described in Additional file 33.
   The separate-site work has two further limitations beside the simulated sites (Sec-
tion 5.4). It depends on the collaborator’s implementation of the published process.
That process trains a student on a large separate public set, a role the training molecules
take here as a compromise, with held-out molecules excluded from it.

Against ensembles, and many representations against few

With the same downstream model on every representation, an ensemble of one model
per representation beats the hybrid with both models on most antibody datasets with
a result (Table 5.15). On the anti-VEGF expression scan the DNN ensemble is ahead
of the hybrid on every fold. On small molecules the hybrid holds up better: the
same-model ensemble beats it with both models only on hERG blocking, and on the
Buchwald–Hartwig reactions. The hybrid is ahead of both ensembles on QM9 with the
DNN and on hERG Ki with the SVM, on every fold. On antibodies the representations
are language models of one sequence that make similar predictions, and averaging them
smooths out the noise of each model on a small training set. On small molecules the
representations carry different information, and one model on their selected features
can use interactions that an average of separate models never sees.
   At the same budget, drawing on several representations beats drawing on one (Ta-
ble 5.16; every budget in Additional file 25). Filled from the top-ranked representation
alone, the budget scores worse than the search’s hybrid at every budget searched, with
both models, on most datasets with a result, and at all but one budget on the rest.

at every budget. The gain comes more from which representations are drawn on, and

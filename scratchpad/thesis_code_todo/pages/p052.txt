The PDV representation is a vector of 200 physicochemical descriptors computed with
RDKit’s MolecularDescriptorCalculator [31, 32]; the 200 are listed in Additional
file 2, and the list is fixed in our code rather than taken from RDKit’s default descrip-
tor set, which grows between releases. ChemBERTa embeddings are the mean over
the non-padding token embeddings of the DeepChem/ChemBERTa-77M-MTR checkpoint,
producing 384 features [28]. MHG-GNNs are a GIN-based autoencoder pre-trained on
about 1.38 million PubChem molecules using β-VAE loss, producing 1024-dimensional
embeddings through iterative message passing and found in the published mhggnn_
pretrained_model_0724_2023 checkpoint [9]. We z-score normalized PDV, Chem-
BERTa and MHG-GNN per feature, using the training molecules’ mean and standard
deviation; ECFP4, Avalon and Sort & Slice reach the model as built.


3.2.3     Models
To understand model behavior in the presence of label noise, we explored a range of
model families. We fitted tree ensembles, a support vector machine, Gaussian processes
and neural networks. Several are variants of one another, differing in the probabilistic
machinery they add, and Additional file 2 gives every configuration with its settings.
   Random Forests (RFs) are one of the most common choices for QSAR model-
ing thanks to their robustness and interpretability [112]. Quantile Regression Forests
(QRFs) hold the distribution of training labels in each leaf and compute quantiles over
all trees [113]. While we used 100 trees for RF, we observed a noisy quantile estimate
with 100 trees for QRF, prompting us to use 300 instead. To observe the effect of
the quantile mechanism, we then fit a second RF at 300 trees so the two models were
identical in all other settings.
   We also used eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) [59], Light Gradient-Boosting
Machine (LightGBM) [62], and Natural Gradient Boosting (NGBoost) [63]. NGBoost
extends deterministic gradient boosting by treating the parameters of a chosen para-
metric distribution as regression targets and learns them through boosting with a
natural gradient update rule [63]. NGBoost relies on scoring off validation data, which
differs by dataset. On QM9 the validation split is already held out of every fit. How-
ever, on the three assay datasets the validation set is a scaffold-grouped fifth of the
rows NGBoost is handed, which already exclude the validation molecules.
   Support vector machines (SVMs) are a well-established baseline in QSAR modeling
[17, 64]. We used a radial basis function (RBF) with C = 1.0 and γ = scale as the
kernel, as it accommodates continuous vectors.

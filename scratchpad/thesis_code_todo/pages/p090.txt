Molecules with identical feature vectors

A standard fingerprint- or descriptor-based representation can produce the same fea-
ture vector for two separate molecules. In turn, models using this representation will
produce a single prediction for the molecules with identical features. Identical feature
vectors do not always mean that the representation has merged two different molecules,
since a dataset pooled from several sources can hold two measurements of the same
molecule.
   Many fingerprints are computed without stereochemistry [124]. In this research,
ECFP4 is built with stereochemistry off, and the 2D descriptor sets (PDV, Mordred)
also ignore it, so almost every molecule they produce identical representations for is
a stereoisomer of another (Table 4.1). Any representation that reads from these sub-
sequently merges stereoisomers. Additionally, methods using the Weisfeiler–Lehman
relabeling scheme are bounded by the 1-Weisfeiler–Lehman test (Chapter 2), so some
non-isomorphic graphs also receive the same vector. This is seen in the graph-kernel
features which tie 105 of 1,412 hERG Ki molecules, 56 of which differ only in stereo-
chemistry and 49 in more than that.
   Whether or not a learned embedding is able to differentiate stereoisomers is de-
pendent on its input as well as its individual implementation. SMI-TED, a pretrained
SMILES encoder, ties 56 hERG Ki , 633 LogD and 177 Caco-2 molecules, all stereoiso-
mers. A tokenizer can drop stereochemistry prior to training. The tokenizer of the
ChemBERTa-77M-MTR checkpoint (ChemBERTa-2 in Additional file 12, called Chem-
BERTa in Chapter 3) gives 72 of the 1,415 hERG Ki molecules of Chapter 3 the same
token sequence as another molecule. Since stereoisomers can differ in measured activity
[125], these representations lack potentially useful chemical information.
   Two molecules are tied when a representation gives them exactly the same feature
vector. Table 4.1 counts ties on hERG Ki , LogD and Caco-2 for each dataset’s base
representations and for its augmentation features, the small feature sets added to the
hybrid after it is built (Section 5.2.3). No tie came from one compound listed twice
(checked by InChIKey, which differs between stereoisomers) or from a row of zeros.
   Apart from the MACCS keys, most tied molecules are stereoisomers. Atom pairs,
PDV, Mordred and SMI-TED tie only stereoisomers, and ECFP4, Avalon and topo-
logical torsions add a few molecules that differ in more than their stereochemistry.

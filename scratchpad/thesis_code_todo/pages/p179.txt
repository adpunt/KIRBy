6.3.3     Activity cliffs
We report activity cliffs on the five small-molecule datasets only. The antibody datasets
have too few held-out cliff antibodies for a score; on ∆∆G, 11 of the 429 held out over
five folds. The rule for held-out molecules is looser than the one for training molecules,
so the share of cliff molecules differs between the two. It is highest on LogD and hERG
blocking, nearly half of the held-out molecules in some folds, and lowest on QM9.
   On LogD, Caco-2, hERG Ki and hERG blocking, each model’s first hybrid beats
its best single representation on cliff molecules and on the other molecules (Table 6.13).
On Caco-2, hERG Ki and hERG blocking the gain is larger on cliff molecules; on Caco-
2 the network goes from 0.340 to 0.288 RMSE on them. The gain on cliff molecules is
slightly smaller on LogD, but both models gain on them in every fold. A second run
with the small-molecule models flags the same cliff molecules and agrees in direction
on all four datasets.
   On QM9 the hybrid is better on the other molecules for both models (Table 6.13).
Only two folds hold the 10 cliff molecules a score needs. In those folds the hybrid is
worse on cliff molecules for both models, for the network from 0.242 to 0.280 RMSE.

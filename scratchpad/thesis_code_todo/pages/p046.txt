Regression noise

The regression injector sets a noise shape (Gaussian, Student-t or Laplace) and a tar-
geting rule (uniform, grouped-wider, grouped-shifted, outlier or censoring) separately.
Each named condition is one combination of the two with its parameters, and the
name is recorded with every result. The requested noise τ is the noise level times the
standard deviation of the clean training labels. The injector solves for the scale τ /G in
closed form (Equation 2.4), and a level of zero returns the labels unchanged. The af-
fected molecules are selected by a separately seeded random number generator, so they
are the same at every noise level while the noise draws change. Under grouped-wider,
whole scaffold families are added in random order until they cover as close as possible
to 20% of the training molecules.
   Censoring is parameterized by the fraction f of labels clipped, with its limit at the
(1 − f ) quantile of the clean training labels. On the training split the censored set is
the k = round(f n) largest labels; on any other split it is whichever molecules lie past
the training limit.
   Each injection also returns a record of the target and delivered root-mean-square
noise, the factor G, the solved scale, the mean of ϵ, the fraction of molecules affected,
the group sizes and the random seeds. The delivered noise is checked against a tolerance
band, and a draw outside it raises a warning (Additional file 1).
   A second function returns the noise scale each molecule receives, without drawing
noise; we apply it to held-out molecules. Under uniform and grouped-shifted targeting
every molecule gets the same scale, so the package flags these scales as degenerate
instead of reporting a rank correlation of zero. A separate injector sizes the noise
against a unit supplied by the user; in Chapter 7 this unit is the standard deviation
of a model’s out-of-fold residuals on the clean training labels. It either adds Gaussian
noise, with variance the level times the square of the unit, or shifts 2%, 5% or 10% of
labels. Each shift is the level times the unit, with a random sign or upward only. It
can also add Gaussian noise whose standard deviation follows a per-molecule covariate
or a hidden log-normal draw, scaled to the same mean variance.

Chapter 6

Similarity in an adaptive hybrid
representation

6.1     Introduction
Chapter 5 built the adaptive hybrid representation, a set of features selected from
several base representations for one task, as input to a model. Chapter 4 showed that
in most standard representations the distance between two molecules barely rises with
the difference in their labels. Any method that trusts a molecule’s neighbors inherits
this, such as predicting a label from the nearest neighbors or finding a noisy label
by comparing it with its neighbors’ labels. The first idea behind the hybrid was to
compute distances on the features relevant to the task alone, and this chapter tests
that idea.
   A hybrid can be built for similarity in two ways. In the first, the search of Chapter 5
uses a nearest-neighbor model as its downstream model, and we measure distances in
the hybrid it selects. In the second, the search ranks each candidate hybrid by how well
each molecule’s five nearest neighbors in it predict its label, and fits no downstream
model. We ask two research questions:

  1. Does distance in a hybrid track label differences better than distance in the best
      single representation?

  2. Do the methods built on that distance work better in a hybrid? We test pre-
      diction from nearest neighbors, activity cliffs, Bayesian optimization, distance to
      the training molecules as a guide to prediction error, retrieval of neighbors and
      clustering.

7.3.5    Effect by noise type
Labels clipped at an assay limit

Clipping at an assay limit is the damage we could most often undo, and undoing it
needs no detector. With a quarter of the training labels clipped (Figure 7.7), for every
model and configuration censored EM gives a lower held-out RMSE than training on
the clipped labels, but not as low as training on the labels before clipping. On LogD,
the network on the best single representation wins back about three quarters of the lost
RMSE on outer fold 0 (Equation 7.6). QM9 shows the same pattern for the network
and LightGBM, and Caco-2 at a smaller scale.
   The gain holds on the further folds: on LogD folds 0 to 4, censored EM improves
the network’s hybrid over the clipped labels on every fold, and stays short of the score
before clipping. It also beats the clipped labels on Caco-2 folds 1 and 2 (Table 7.19).
hERG Ki and QM9 have only fold 0.

5.2.8    Models and metrics
The downstream models (Chapter 2) are random forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, an
SVM, a DNN and a Gaussian process [115]. Each dataset uses the two that scored
best in the earlier search of the whole dataset (Table 5.6). All six are used: the Gaus-
sian process is one of the two models on four small antibody datasets (angiopoietin-2
binding, HIC retention, AC-SINS self-association and CHO polyreactivity). Tree en-
sembles are the baseline to beat, because a hybrid is a tabular feature matrix and
tree-based models still lead on medium-sized tabular data of about 10,000 samples
[236].
   By default, every model uses the same settings on every dataset, so that score
differences come from the input. An optional per-fold random search tries 20 draws of
a model’s settings on 3 group-aware inner folds of the outer training fold. In the search,
the DNN is not tuned this way and relies on early stopping instead. On one outer fold
of five datasets, tuning helped the SVM most and made the random forest worse on
Buchwald–Hartwig yield (Additional file 23). The Gaussian process and the SVM are
skipped above the dataset sizes in Table 5.5, because their fits no longer finish in useful
time. Other models left out on named datasets are listed in Additional file 14, and all
model settings are in Additional file 13.
   The kNN model is not a default downstream model, but a third downstream model
in the search with the nearest-neighbor objective (Chapter 6), on every dataset. It
takes k = 5 neighbors by default, lowered to the square root of the number of training
samples when a fold has fewer than 25.
   Each dataset is scored in the metric its field normally uses, so that it can be com-
pared with published work (Table 5.7). Regression on small molecules, QM9 included,
and on reaction yields uses RMSE in the label’s units. Antibody regression uses Spear-
man ρ, because these assays rank candidates and their scales differ between laborato-
ries. Binary classification uses AUROC, which does not depend on a decision threshold.
We do not average metrics across datasets.
   Each comparison is reported as its per-fold differences and their spread across the 5
folds. We use no significance tests and no confidence intervals, because 5 fold scores are
correlated through shared training molecules. No unbiased estimator of the variance
of k-fold cross-validation exists that is valid under all distributions, and estimates that
ignore the overlap underestimate it [237].

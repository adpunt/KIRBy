Chapter 7

Detecting and handling label noise

7.1     Introduction
In Chapter 6, on the five small-molecule datasets, distance in a hybrid built for distance
tracked the label more closely than distance in a single representation. Prediction from
nearest neighbors also improved on all five. This chapter asks whether that helps with
label noise. Can we find the noisy labels in a QSAR dataset, and does acting on them
improve a model’s held-out score? We ask this on two kinds of labels. The first has
damage injected on purpose, so we know which labels were damaged and by how much.
The second is the labels as delivered, which carry assay error that nobody has marked.
   Every method in this chapter reads a feature space.          A detector compares a
molecule’s label with its neighbors’ labels, or with a model’s prediction, and both
depend on the features. The adaptive hybrid representation (Chapter 5) leaves out
features that do not relate to the label yet add a term to the distance (Chapter 4).
For every method we therefore ask whether it improves the held-out score and, where
it does, whether it improves the hybrid more than the best single representation.
   The methods fall into the three groups of Table 2.3. This chapter expands the
sweep of twelve published methods in Chapter 4 (Section 4.3.3). It also adds methods
that select molecules by a known flag, such as a label at an assay limit, which none of
the published methods uses. QM9, whose labels are computed rather than measured,
is the clean-label control (Section 7.2.5).

choosing which molecules to measure, a form of Bayesian optimization. Starting from
a set of whole scaffold families holding at least 5% of the molecules, a surrogate model
picks 100 molecules one at a time by expected improvement [253]. We repeat this
from three starting sets, and the score is the area under the best-found-so-far curve.
Last, for each representation a predictive hybrid draws from, we compare the label
information in the features it kept against a random subset of the same size. Each
block of features is reduced to at most 50 principal components. A mutual-information
estimate is summed over the components, and the mean over three label permutations
is subtracted. We use scikit-learn’s estimators, which follow Kraskov et al. [254] for
continuous labels and Ross [255] for classes. This sum counts information shared by
two components twice.


6.3        Results
We first ask whether distance in a hybrid tracks label differences more closely than in a
single representation, and then whether the methods built on that distance gain from
it.

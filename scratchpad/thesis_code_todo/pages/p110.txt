   The hybrid search returns a list of settings for feature selection, referred to here as
a configuration. It is scored by fitting the downstream model on the hybrid in each
fold of the search and averaging the held-out scores. Each pair of downstream model
and budget has its own search, and thus its own winner. Because this score is chosen
over a range of selected configurations, it is optimistic [184, 185]. However, so is its
direct comparison with the highest-performing base representation on the dataset. The
whole search runs inside each outer training fold, and the winning settings are rebuilt
there and scored once on the untouched outer test fold, both for the winner at each
budget and for each downstream model’s best across budgets. The inner search score
minus the outer test score is reported against the budget in Section 5.3.1.
   Two variants of this search are used later: under label noise, training quantities use
noisy labels while held-out scoring uses clean labels (Chapter 7). Under the nearest-
neighbor objective, each sample’s label is predicted from its five nearest neighbors in
the feature space (Chapter 6).

Activity cliffs

Chapter 2 gives the MoleculeACE definition of an activity cliff, which combines three
similarity measures. Here we use one. Two molecules are similar if their ECFP4
Tanimoto similarity is at least 0.55 [145], and a molecule is a cliff if a similar molecule
has a label that differs from its own by more than a set gap. We chose the gap on each
dataset so that 15% of the molecules are cliffs, so the gap follows the spread of the
labels (Table 4.2). On hERG Ki the gap is 1.76 pKi , and 211 of the 1,412 molecules
are cliffs. On QM9 only 1,093 pairs of the 9,978 molecules are similar, and the gap is
0.05 eV, 0.06 times the median absolute deviation of the labels. On the three Biogen
datasets (RLM clearance, MDR1–MDCK efflux and HLM clearance) it is 0.20 to 0.34
log units, 0.50 to 0.60 times the median absolute deviation.
   The count of cliffs depends on the representation. We kept each dataset’s gap and
replaced ECFP4 with each other base representation, calling similar the same number
of closest pairs as ECFP4 does. On hERG Ki the number of cliff molecules then ranges
from 138 (atom pairs) to 267 (WL graph kernel). Of the pairs that ECFP4 calls similar,
only 57% to 74% are also among another representation’s closest pairs, and on QM9
only 1% to 24% (Additional file S.3).


Distance and label difference

We asked whether distance tracks label difference in two ways. Over all pairs of
molecules, we took the Spearman correlation between the distance of a pair and the
absolute difference of its labels, a one-number summary of the neighborhood-behavior
plot of Patterson et al. [146]; datasets above 2,000 molecules were sampled down to
2,000. Over each molecule’s closest pairs only, we predicted each label as the mean
label of the molecule’s five nearest neighbors, taken from other scaffold groups, and
took the Spearman correlation between predicted and measured labels.

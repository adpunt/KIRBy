   In an early study with Gaussian noise added to QM9 labels, the published methods
for noisy labels that compared molecules by a distance did worst. We hypothesized
that the distance was at fault. On a given dataset, most features of a general-purpose
representation either do not vary or do not relate to the property. Yet each adds a
term to the distance, so together they dominate it (Chapter 4). We propose instead
to compute distances over a hybrid representation: a fixed number of task-relevant
features selected from several representations. A first version showed that the selected
features work as ordinary model inputs too, so the question became how to choose
them (Chapter 5). Chapter 6 tests whether distance in the hybrid tracks the label
better, and Chapter 7 returns to noisy labels, comparing the single representations
with the hybrid. The same problem appears in antibody representations, where the
features that matter are few and buried in representations of the whole sequence. For
this reason we test the method on antibodies, and on chemical reactions.

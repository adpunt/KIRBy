QM9 with no noise added comes from a change that would help on any labels, such as
a better-trained model. The result we look for is a method that leaves QM9 unchanged
and improves a measured dataset’s labels as delivered. That is the strongest evidence
we have that a method found real noise. A gain on the labels as delivered that QM9
matches is not such evidence. The shuffled-score control cannot make this distinction
alone, because it asks whether the choice of molecules matters, not whether there
is noise. We therefore set each method’s result on QM9 beside its gains elsewhere
(Section 7.3.2).

7.3     Results
Three things hold for every result in this chapter. First, because of time and compute,
most results are on the first outer fold only (Section 5.2.5), and we give the fold count
wherever more exist. Second, where a method has several settings, such as a share
to delete or a strength, we report its best setting on the held-out score; because the
single representation and the hybrid are picked the same way, every reported gain is
optimistic. Third, the network’s score changes from one fit to the next. On QM9
its second hybrid scores between 0.348 and 0.362 RMSE on the same fold and labels,
depending on which experiment fitted it, whereas LightGBM gives one score in every
experiment. A network gain of about 0.01 RMSE or less is inside that spread.

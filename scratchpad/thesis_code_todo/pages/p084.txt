   Third, it is hard to draw conclusions that hold across so many conditions. No model
or representation wins everywhere, although RF comes closest. When working with
cheminformatics data, you don’t know in advance how a model and representation will
perform on the given prediction task. The underlying data is probably noisy, but you
don’t know how noisy it is or what shape that noise takes.
   Given that, our recommendation is to test a handful of models against a handful of
representations rather than tuning a single pairing, since on every dataset the pairing
explains more of the variance in robustness than the representation does alone. The
pattern of the noise should also guide that choice. The same preferences held on all
four datasets. Under Laplace, Student-t and outlier noise, RF was the most robust
model and SVM was the second. NGBoost lost AUCnorm under all three. Under
grouped-wider noise, QRF was the most robust model and SVM was close behind.
Of the boosted trees, XGBoost lost the most. Grouped-shifted noise was the hardest
condition. Every model lost AUCnorm on every dataset and representation. RF lost the
least of the models that also predicted well, and the GP lost the most. Gaussian noise
was the only condition with a clear specialist. NGBoost was the most robust model on
the HOMO–LUMO gap under Gaussian noise with every representation, but not on
Caco-2 or hERG Ki .
   These results follow from the loss function each model fits. NGBoost fits a per-
molecule scale under a Gaussian likelihood, so it loses ground when the noise is not
Gaussian. SVM ignores residuals that fall inside its margin, so it gains under the
same conditions. QRF fits conditional quantiles, so a noisy scaffold family widens that
family’s interval without shifting the fit. Each boosting stage fits the residuals of the
stage before it, and those residuals are largest in the noisy families. The GP is the
most affected by error that is correlated with structure, because its kernel measures
structure. None of this matters if the data are clean, as little separated the models
below a noise level of 0.3.
   In this chapter each model was given a standard molecular representation, and the
choice of representation explained relatively little of the variance in noise robustness.
We observe that this choice does impact the performance of different models to varying
degrees, and the representations seem to capture different signals on each dataset that
we tested. Chapter 4 asks how well these representations compare molecules on a single
dataset.

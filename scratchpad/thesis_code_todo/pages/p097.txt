A small sweep of published noise-handling methods

In Chapter 3, model family explained the largest share of variance in noise robust-
ness, and representation at most 8.1% on QM9 (Table 3.1). Earlier in this project
we therefore ran a small sweep of published noise-handling methods, to ask whether
training methods built for noisy labels improve robustness and whether representation
still matters under them.
   The three groups of methods for noisy labels and the five categories of Song et al.
[51] are described in Section 2.5.2, and such methods have been studied across ma-
chine learning, including QSAR. Most methods in the LNL literature were built for
image classification with flipped labels [51], so they do not transfer automatically to
continuous labels.
   The study tested 12 published noise-handling methods on the QM9 HOMO–LUMO
gap (10,000 molecules, scaffold splits), adding Gaussian noise with σ from 0 to 1.0 in
steps of 0.1 to the training labels only. The representations were ECFP4, Sort & Slice
[7], PDV, canonical SMILES and randomized SMILES. Depending on the method,
a configuration could also add the model’s own uncertainty or a distance between

   Three optional wrappers produce ui from a point predictor: split-conformal predic-
tion around any scikit-learn-compatible model, optionally scaled by the label spread of
each molecule’s nearest training neighbors; an exact Gaussian process with a Tanimoto
or RBF kernel through Gauche [68]; and Monte Carlo dropout on any PyTorch network
[104].


2.7      Evaluation methodology
Chapter 3 reports predictive performance as R2 on held-out molecules and robustness
as AUCnorm (Equation 3.2). Chapters 4 to 7 state the metric used for each dataset
where it is introduced, and report results as per-fold scores and their spread across
folds, with no significance tests.
   No split or fold in this thesis is random, and no group of molecules is split between
training and test. Random splitting inflates performance estimates for molecular data,
because it places structurally similar molecules on both sides of the train/test boundary
[105–107]. For small molecules the group is the Bemis–Murcko scaffold family (Sec-
tion 2.5.1). Section 3.2 gives the splits of Chapter 3, and Section 5.2.6 gives the folds
of the later chapters and the groups used for datasets that are not small molecules.


2.8      Conclusions
A predictor has two parts, the representation and the model. The representation also
shapes what counts as similar, since similarity depends on both the representation and
the coefficient. Activity cliffs and uninformative features, in turn, break the similar
property principle. Real assay noise is often neither independent nor identically dis-
tributed, and it need not be zero-mean. For this reason we defined seven artificial
noise conditions. Methods for noisy labels fall into noise detection, noise mitigation
and noise-robust training. Whichever group a method belongs to, none is expected to
win on every dataset. We implemented the seven conditions in NoiseInject, an open-
source package that injects them and reports the robustness and uncertainty metrics
for any model. Chapter 3 asks whether the representation or the model architecture
governs how strongly a predictor degrades under label noise, through the questions set
out in Chapter 1.

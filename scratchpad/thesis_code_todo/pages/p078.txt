Aleatoric and epistemic attribution

While this tells us that the models are reportedly less certain under noisy data, we
don’t know what the model is attributing this uncertainty to. To answer this question,
we decompose uncertainty estimates into their aleatoric and epistemic components
wherever possible. We expect to see aleatoric uncertainty rise with injected label
noise, while the epistemic component should remain relatively constant, as it is the
aleatoric component that describes uncertainty in the underlying data. The models
whose frameworks allowed for this decomposition include full-BNNs with a variance
head, the heteroscedastic GP, and QRF (Table 3.5). The two heteroscedastic VBLL
networks also report both components, and they separate them more often than any
other model, though not on every dataset and representation. They are not drawn in
the figures, as they were not scored out of fold.
   The full-BNNs with a variance head behave as expected (Figure 3.13), the epistemic
component grows by at most 0.43 of the aleatoric growth in every configuration where
the ratio can be computed. Despite QRF’s strong predictive performance and noise
robustness, both of its aleatoric and epistemic components rise with noise and on some
representations, the epistemic component actually rises at a faster rate.

similarity search, described in Section 6.2.2. From these three searches, we take the
following feature spaces:

   • the nearest-neighbor model’s best and second-best single representations, ranked
     by the distance search;

   • each downstream model’s best and second-best single representations, ranked by
     its general search;

   • each downstream model’s two best hybrids, four in all: the predictive hybrids;

   • the top hybrid of the distance search: the distance hybrid;

   • the top hybrid of the similarity search: the similarity hybrid, on the datasets
     where that search is defined.

   Unless stated otherwise, “the single representation” in the results means the nearest-
neighbor model’s best single representation, and “the hybrid” means the distance hy-
brid. Retrieval and clustering also score one control: the principal components of all
representations joined, with as many components as the hybrid has features.


6.2.2    Similarity in a hybrid
The search of Chapter 5 can be written as follows. Each configuration θ fixes an
importance method, an allocation method, a number of representations and a budget
B. Given base representations R1 , . . . , Rm , a configuration builds a feature set Sθ ⊂
R1 ∪ · · · ∪ Rm with |Sθ | ≤ B. The search returns Sθ∗ , where θ∗ = arg maxθ s(Sθ ) and
s is the search’s score. In the general search, s is a downstream model’s score on the
search’s own held-out folds. In the similarity search, s(S) = q5 (S), how accurately
each molecule’s label is predicted from its five nearest neighbors in S (Equation 6.1),
and no downstream model is fitted. All other settings and the folds are shared with
the general search. The neighbor score, computed on a validation set taken from the
fold’s training molecules, also replaces the internal evaluator when the budget is split.
   Zheng and Tropsha [134] and Itskowitz and Tropsha [135] chose descriptors to
maximize nearest-neighbor prediction within one descriptor set, and we modify this by
choosing features across several representations. The similarity hybrid tests whether
choosing features for how well the neighbors predict a label does better than choosing
them for a model’s prediction.

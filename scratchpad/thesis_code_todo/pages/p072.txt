it falls. The choice of representation has little impact on GP, moving its AUCnorm less
than repeating the split does on every dataset.
   There is a slight consideration with using both AUCnorm and predictive performance
side-by-side. AUCnorm measures the fraction of its own clean R2 that a model keeps,
so predictive performance on clean labels works against it by construction. A model
that already predicts poorly has little to lose and often scores a higher AUCnorm , while
a model that starts with strong performance has more to lose. The GP and NGBoost
are the clearest cases. The GP has among the highest predictive performance on clean
labels and a middling AUCnorm , while NGBoost has the highest AUCnorm across the
board from one of the lowest clean R2 values. While NGBoost’s predictive performance
is highly dependent on its representation, its robustness is not. On QM9 it is both the
most robust model and the lowest ranked model for predictive performance up to a
noise level of 0.75 with every representation. But with PDV it’s able to retain some
predictive performance under noise, finishing highest across all the models at noise
level 1.5.
   Upon first glance, SVM does not seem like a standout, however, it is the model
whose standing is better with non-Gaussian noise. Among RF, the GP, NGBoost
and SVM, the four models run under every condition, SVM has the lowest AUCnorm
of the four under Gaussian noise on almost every dataset and representation, and
rises to second under Laplace, Student-t and outlier noise and under grouped-wider
noise. Gaussian noise is actually the one condition it is worst at. Its AUCnorm stays
in the middle, partially because it starts high, and it ranks among the five highest
for predictive performance at a noise level on every assay dataset under every noise
condition.
   The feed-forward networks are the most variable, although in general they have
some of the highest predictive performance under clean labels and are generally not
robust to noise. They are also dependent on the choice of representation. VBLL
networks perform poorly on clean labels, are the least robust to noise on LogD, yet are
the most robust on Caco-2 and hERG Ki . The gradient-boosted trees, LightGBM and
XGBoost, were also quite variable, with strong predictive performance on clean labels
and poor noise robustness on Caco-2 and hERG Ki .
   The quantity of noise is an important factor as well. At a noise level of 0.5 the
models still rank by predictive performance much as they do on clean labels, while
by 1.0 the order has changed, most of all on hERG Ki and then QM9. The order of
models by AUCnorm is broadly similar across noise conditions, with the forests near the
top under each.

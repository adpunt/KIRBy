2.5.1      Artificial noise injection
Our objective is to evaluate the robustness of QSAR models against label noise. To do
so, we “inject” artificial noise into the labels during training while test data remain free
of noise. Validation labels are given noise of the same type and quantity as the training
labels, drawn independently. This artificial noise simulates real-world experimental
noise. The clean target label yi is replaced by a noisy label ỹi = yi + ϵi , where ϵi
is the artificial noise injected at the index i, the value of which is determined by a
specified noise strategy. As we increased the quantity of noise, we measured how much
predictive performance fell.
     We explored seven different ways of adding noise, which we refer to as noise con-
ditions and which are illustrated in Figure 2.2 and summarized in Table 2.2. Three
conditions vary the shape of the distribution from which the error is drawn, and three
concentrate the error on some molecules or share it within Bemis–Murcko scaffold
families. The seventh condition, censoring, clips labels at a limit.
     Molecules that share a Bemis–Murcko scaffold, the ring systems and linking chains
left once all other side chains are removed [97], form a scaffold family.

   Although the general assumption in much of the noise analysis and mitigation lit-
erature is that noise, specifically label noise, will have a negative effect on the model,
this is not necessarily the case. There are multiple instances of using noise as a method
of regularization. In linear regression, appending observations of pure noise shrinks
the coefficient estimates, an implicit regularization, although not an optimal one [95].
Furthermore, adding artificial noise drawn from a Gaussian distribution to the activa-
tion layers of a neural network is considered another form of regularization [96]. These
studies indicate that the presence of noise may act as a form of regularization in some
contexts. It is important to address this potential of regularization from label noise in
QSAR and quantitative structure–property relationship (QSPR) models.

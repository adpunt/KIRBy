Chemical reactions

Reaction datasets (Table 5.10) use three kinds of representation. The condensed graph
of reaction (CGR) merges reactants and products into one graph in which the bonds
that change are marked [203, 204]. Here it is hashed into a circular fingerprint after
atom mapping [205]. Reaction language models read the reaction SMILES directly
[206, 207]. Any molecular representation can also be applied to the reactants, to the
product, or to their difference. A one-hot encoding of which aryl halide, ligand, base
and additive was used carries no chemistry. It is kept because on plate data such
identity features can predict yield about as well as chemical descriptors [208].


Antibodies

Antibody representations (Table 5.11) start from the heavy- and light-chain amino-
acid sequences. Besides one-hot and BLOSUM62 encodings [209], most are frozen
language models, either trained on all proteins or on antibody repertoires only. Most
models embed each chain separately and join the two vectors, so their width doubles on
datasets with both chains. Several representations read one model in different ways, for
instance averaging over earlier layers or over the complementarity-determining region
(CDR) residues only, with CDRs found by IMGT numbering [210] using ANARCI
[211]. Datasets of point variants of one parent antibody also use features that describe
each mutation.

Ensembles

The ensemble fits one model per representation and pools their predictions (late fusion),
whereas the hybrid fits one model on one fused feature vector (early fusion) [165]. Every
choice for the ensemble is made on an inner 3-fold group split of the outer training
samples. The first ensemble uses the same downstream model on every representation,
and the second uses each representation’s own better model. Each picks its members,
the top 3 representations or all of them, and one of seven ways to pool predictions,
from the mean and median to trimmed, outlier-removed and score-weighted means.


Many or few representations

On each outer fold, the base representations are ranked by their summed feature im-
portance on the training samples. The top 1, 2 or 3 of them supply the B features
with the highest importance among their own features. These are compared with the
search’s own hybrid at B and with an even split of B over every base representation,
each using the importance method that the fold’s best hybrid used. The search’s hy-
brid differs from the hand-built hybrids in two ways at once. It ranks representations
by their standalone scores rather than by summed importance, and it stops at the last
feature with importance above zero.


Easy and hard halves

Datasets above 2,000 samples are first reduced to about 2,000 by drawing whole scaffold
families with a fixed seed. Scaffolds at or above the 80th percentile of group size form
the easy half, and the rest form the hard half. Each half gets its own search on each
outer fold. The hybrid of a search on all the subset’s training samples is also rebuilt and
scored on each half. Three other splits were run as well (by heavy-atom count, label
spread within a scaffold and feature spread within a scaffold), which are not reported
here.

7.3.9    Effect by model and representation
Cleaning keeps the hybrid ahead on small molecules. Before any treatment the hybrid
already beats the best single representation in almost every small-molecule condition,
and after mitigation it still does. On LogD with outliers, for example, the network’s
cleaned hybrid scores 0.556 against 0.591 for the cleaned single representation (Ta-
ble 7.10). Cleaning rarely changes which of the two wins, and when it does it more
often moves the win to the hybrid. On antibodies the hybrid is ahead in about half
the conditions, before and after cleaning. The reaction set sits between the two.
   The closest test of the question this thesis started from, whether a better feature set
makes noise handling work, is where the detector finds its neighbors. One experiment
down-weights molecules by the neighbor gap and finds the neighbors either in the
model’s own single representation or in the hybrid, with everything else held fixed.
Finding them in the hybrid helped the same model on the same single representation
slightly more often. The largest differences go both ways. On LogD under a scaffold-
family shift, the network’s single representation goes from 0.705 to 0.663 RMSE with
neighbors in the hybrid and gets worse with neighbors in its own space. On nanobody
thermostability with outliers, the network barely moves with its own neighbors and
gains with the hybrid’s. On expression titer under Gaussian noise the reverse happens,
and its own neighbors help more than the hybrid’s (Table 7.34). These are the largest
cases picked out of many, not typical ones, and no control has run for this comparison.

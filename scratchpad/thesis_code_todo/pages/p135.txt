nor on multiclass tasks. The five conditions are:

1. No weighting: the features as selected.

2. Feature weighting: standardized features multiplied by the square root of their
   importance, with the weights normalized to average 1.

3. Sample weighting: samples weighted by how well their label agrees with their 10
   nearest neighbors in the rescaled space.

4. Combined weighting: feature and sample weighting together.

5. Above-median selection: only the features strictly above the median importance.

The weight of sample i is 1 − |yi − ȳN (i) |/(max y − min y), clipped to lie between 0.1
and 1, where ȳN (i) is the mean label of its 10 nearest neighbors by Manhattan distance.
Tree splits depend only on the order of values [238], so for tree models feature weighting
differs from no weighting only in that zero-importance features drop out. The rescaling
does change which samples are nearest neighbors, and so changes sample weighting.
Sample weighting down-weights rather than removes a suspect label, unlike neighbor-
based filtering (Section 4.3.3). We found no QSAR study that does this, and learned
per-sample weights for noisy labels come from general machine learning [239].


Fewer features or a tuned model

For the SVM and the random forest, the best hybrid at a small budget is fitted at
the model’s default setting. It is compared with a hybrid rebuilt at the largest width
we allow: the smallest of the pool width, twice the number of training samples and
2,048 features. This wider hybrid is fitted at the setting that scores best on the other
folds (the SVM’s C or the forest’s maximum depth). Datasets whose two downstream
models include neither family are not run.


Adding a new embedding

Each embedding of the pool is taken in turn as the new one, and the search is run
with it and without it. As a control, the embedding’s selected features in the hybrid
are replaced by the same number of Gaussian random features, with no new search.
On LogD and hERG blocking the experiment ran on 2,000 molecules, drawn as whole
scaffold families.

   ANOVA relies on the assumption that observations both within and across groups
are independent. Two features of this study break that assumption. First, each QM9
replicate uses the same data split and the same noise draw for every model and rep-
resentation, so observations from one replicate are not independent of each other; we
therefore entered the replicate as a block. Second, across the whole set of models in
this study, some pairs produced similar predictions that are not independent, so a
method present in several near-identical versions would count several times towards
the share of model architecture. To handle this issue, we grouped the models into
families. We considered grouping pairs of models that share a method or an archi-
tecture, and compared each pair on clean R2 and on AUCnorm under the three noise
conditions run on every representation, separately on each of the six representations,
which gives 24 comparisons per pair. A pair was grouped when the mean difference
between the two models was smaller than their standard deviation across replicates
in at least two thirds of these comparisons (Additional file 4). Five families met this
criterion: RF with quantile regression forests (QRF; 16 of 24), XGBoost with Light-
GBM (23 of 24), NN-α with BNN-α (20 of 24), NN-β with BNN-β (18 of 24), and
VBLL-α with VBLL-β (20 of 24). VBLL did not group with the plain or Bayesian
network of its own architecture (4 to 10 of 24), as its clean R2 was lower by 0.02 to
0.09, and NGBoost did not group with XGBoost or LightGBM (2 and 3 of 24). The
two architectures of the plain networks, and of the Bayesian networks, also met the
criterion (18 and 17 of 24); we grouped by architecture instead, which the evidence
supports equally well. The 13 base models therefore form eight families. Within these
families, the differences between models accounted for at most 1.0% of the variance in
any outcome and noise condition on QM9, and up to 15.1% of predictive performance
on clean labels on the three assay datasets. We also ran Tukey’s honestly significant
difference test between the members of each family, separately for each representation

5.3.4     Collaboration studies
The small-molecule result carries over to the three activity-cliff targets of Dablander
et al. [57], data the hybrid was not developed on. For every target and model pair except
factor Xa with LightGBM, the hybrid has a lower error than the single representation
the same search ranked first (Table 5.23). With the DNN on factor Xa it is lower on all
five folds. These targets have many activity cliffs, which is where a single similarity-
based representation struggles most (Chapter 4).

   On Caco-2, MACCS keys and the WL graph kernel both give some molecules a row
that another molecule also has (Table 4.1). Amino-acid composition does the same to
432 of the 659 variants in the EGFR scan, which differ by single mutations. None of
the hybrids built on these datasets leaves two samples with the same row.
   On hERG Ki the graph kernel leaves some molecules sharing a row (Table 4.1).
Adding features from ECFP4 and MHG-GNN lowers its RMSE with the SVM on all
five folds, and no two molecules share a row (Table 5.24). Adding ECFP4 and PDV
instead improves it almost as much while some molecules still share a row. Most of
the gain therefore comes from the information the added representations give every
molecule, and little from breaking the ties.
   Search settings found on LogD were reused on hERG Ki , with the features selected
again on hERG Ki . They beat hERG Ki ’s best single representation with both models
(with the SVM on all five folds), but stay behind a search run on hERG Ki itself
(Table 5.25). Reusing settings saves the search on the smaller dataset at some cost in
error.

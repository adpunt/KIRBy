an alternative GP kernel or loss function, for 221 configurations in all. Four methods
detect suspected molecules, remove them and retrain (noise mitigation): small-loss
selection, confident learning, distance-based selection and multistage cleaning. The
other eight change the training (noise-robust training).
   We measured noise robustness by retention, R2 under noise divided by R2 with
clean labels, as a percentage: the retention ratio of Chapter 3 (Equation 3.2). The
study predates AUCnorm , the area under the retention curve. Retention correlates
more closely with clean R2 , so it separates robustness from accuracy less well, but it
is a good enough proxy for comparing methods with and without a distance. In an
analysis of variance over the adapted components, using a distance explained the most
variance in R2 . All 20 configurations with the lowest retention used a distance, and
none with a distance reached both a clean R2 above 0.6 and a retention above 80%.
   The methods that held up estimate noise from the model’s own loss or uncer-
tainty. DivideMix [150] retained the most, 86.7% (standard deviation 0.4%), followed
by small-loss selection [151] at 84.2% (2.1%) and the uncertainty curriculum, which or-
ders molecules by the model’s own loss [152, 153], at 82.7% (3.5%). The heteroscedas-
tic GP, a GP whose noise is predicted per molecule by a second model [154], retained
79.0%, averaged over 15 configurations, with R2 of 0.71 on clean labels and 0.56 at
σ = 0.6, where its retention was measured. The exploratory study does not record
this noise level for the other methods. Regularization alone retained less, 73.0% for
Mixup and 74.0% for sharpness-aware minimization. Under these methods, the choice
of representation explained even less variance than in Chapter 3.
   The study added Gaussian noise to every training label of a single dataset and
property, the QM9 HOMO–LUMO gap. It also added a distance to methods designed
without one, so poor adaptation is an alternative explanation for their low retention.
Chapter 7 runs a new implementation of the same 12 methods on more datasets, on
hybrids and under the other noise conditions of Section 2.5.1 (Section 7.3.8).
   The distances may have failed for two reasons, which the evidence here does not sep-
arate. First, a single representation may lack the information a distance needs, with
and possibly even without noise. PDVs encode global molecular properties, ECFP4
contains substructure information and MHG-GNNs encode the molecular graph [9],
and any one of these sources alone may not be sufficient for robust similarity estima-
tion. Second, some representations are sparse, with many features irrelevant to the
molecules of a given dataset (Section 4.3.2). We sought to address the second with
the adaptive hybrid representation (Chapter 5), which concatenates the top-ranked
features by importance from several base representations.

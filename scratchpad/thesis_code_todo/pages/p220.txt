A shared offset per scaffold group

Under grouped-shifted noise, subtracting each scaffold family’s estimated offset worked
on all three datasets it ran on, Caco-2, hERG Ki and LogD. On Caco-2 and LogD it
improved the network on the hybrid on every damaged draw, and stayed short of the
score on clean labels (Table 7.22). On the first Caco-2 draw the random-intercept
model took it from 0.321 to 0.280 RMSE, and it was best on every Caco-2 draw and
on the first two LogD draws. On hERG Ki neither of the two was best on every draw.
   On the labels as delivered the same methods made every dataset worse (Table 7.23).
On Caco-2 the network on the hybrid went from 0.233 to 0.267 RMSE after subtracting
family means, and LogD got worse too. Because nothing had shifted a family, sub-
tracting the estimated offsets removed real differences between families. The method
assumes a shared offset per family and should be used only where there is reason to
expect one.

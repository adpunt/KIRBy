   To assess the interaction between uncertainty estimation and label noise, we posed
three questions. First, whether uncertainty rises with label noise, as measured by
the mean ui over held-out molecules at the highest noise level divided by its value
with no added noise, in each replicate or fold. The second pertains to models that
report separate aleatoric and epistemic components. We divided the increase of mean
epistemic uncertainty between the highest level of noise and clean labels by the mean
of the aleatoric component to determine how each responds to label noise. A ratio near
0 means the added noise was attributed to the labels while a ratio of 1 or above means
the epistemic component grew as much or more. We also compared the mean aleatoric
component with the standard deviation of the injected noise. The third question we
had was whether per-sample uncertainty values track injected label noise, as measured
by the Spearman correlation between ui and |ϵi | over out-of-fold training molecules,
per fold and per noise level. We also report the Spearman correlation between ui and
the absolute error of the prediction. Finally, we asked whether dividing a molecule’s
out-of-fold absolute error by ui ranks the corrupted labels better than the error alone,
and report the difference between the two Spearman correlations with |ϵi |.

Overfitting of the search

With budgets searched up to 8,000 features, and at half to twice the number of training
samples, the selection bias is small on the larger small-molecule datasets. On the small
antibody datasets the search promises much more than it delivers (Table 5.18). On
these datasets the folds also often pick a hybrid wider than their own training set, for
example on every fold of EGFR binding with the SVM. The search’s internal scores
can choose between configurations with a few thousand molecules, but not with 150 to
500 training antibodies, where its choice is partly noise.
   On permuted class labels, selecting features on the full dataset before cross-
validation gave Ambroise and McLachlan [182] an error close to zero, against close
to chance with selection redone inside each fold. Every number in this chapter is
scored on samples the search never saw, so the gains above are not this bias. The bias
still exists inside the search, and the numbers above show where it is large: on small
datasets, where many configurations are compared on few samples [246].
   Leaving one more fold of training samples out of each fold’s search makes the
held-out score worse on every small-molecule dataset with a result, with both models
(Table 5.19). On hERG Ki , Caco-2 and LogD it is worse by 0.009 to 0.037 RMSE.
The two searches differ in two ways at once, the training samples and the budget each
one picks, so we cannot say which of the two causes the loss.

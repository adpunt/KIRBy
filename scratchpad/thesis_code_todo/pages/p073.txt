   While it is clear that QSAR model performance drops with label noise, different
models drop faster than others. How much noise a dataset tolerates before anything
happens is itself a property of the dataset. Up to a noise level of 0.3, three tenths of
the spread of the clean labels, the models are largely unaffected by the noise on the
HOMO–LUMO gap and on LogD, where every base model with ECFP4 still keeps at
least 93% of its clean R2 ; on Caco-2 one of the 13 and on hERG Ki four of the 13 are
already below 90% at that level. By a noise level of 0.5 the lowest retained fraction is
90% on the HOMO–LUMO gap against 76% on hERG Ki (Figure 3.9). Boosted tree
ensembles are some of the first to fall. On Caco-2 with ECFP4, LightGBM has among
the highest predictive performance on clean labels. It is also the first tree ensemble to
fall below 90% of that performance, at a noise level of 0.5. By level 1.5 it retains 26%
of its original R2 , compared to 63% for RF on ECFP4. XGBoost drops off at the next
recorded noise level. However, NGBoost is the opposite, having some of the lowest
predictive performance on clean labels and the flattest curve with injected label noise
across all models. On the HOMO–LUMO gap it never falls below 90% of its clean R2
at any level tested. The networks drop at an inconsistent rate. VBLL-α is the earliest
to fall on LogD, dropping below 90% of its clean R2 at a level of 0.5 and reaching 38%
by 1.5. Yet on Caco-2 and hERG Ki it is among the flattest models.
   The variance decomposition of predictive performance tells us how much the choice
of model and representation matters under clean labels (Figure 3.2; Additional file 7).
Extending that concept, we are also interested in how these shares change under in-
creasing label noise and compare to the variance decomposition of AUCnorm . The
replicate and residuals account for more variance across all datasets. On Caco-2 and
hERG Ki the replicate, which on these datasets is one of five scaffold folds, is the
largest share of predictive performance before and after noise is injected. Conversely,
it falls well below the choice of model family for robustness. On every dataset the in-
teraction between model and representation explains more of the variance in AUCnorm
than representation alone.

7.2.2     Noise detection
We group the detectors (Table 2.3) by what they read: an out-of-fold prediction, the
neighbors’ labels, or a model’s predicted spread (Table 7.3). Every detector of the first
two kinds reads the molecule’s own label. Three of the third kind, the heteroscedastic
GP, the variance-head network and NGBoost, predict a spread from the features alone
and never read it. Such a detector can only find a wrong label if the damage is tied
to structure. Most detectors only rank molecules, but a few of the third kind return a
spread in the label’s own units, which can feed a weight or a move.
   The gap over neighbor spread is the buddy check used to screen weather-station
readings, which divides a reading’s gap from its neighbors’ mean by their standard
deviation [256]. RegENN applies a similar threshold to filter noisy regression labels
[257], but we found no earlier use of either on molecular labels. The cliff-aware gap
divides by the spread of all labels plus that of the neighbors. A molecule among
neighbors that disagree with each other, as near an activity cliff, scores lower as a result.
SLOM lowers an outlier score in the same way where a neighborhood is unstable, but
for spatial data, where neighbors are found by location [258].

**Measures.** Each is a standard measure from the linguistics literature. The parser is spaCy 3.8 (`en_core_web_sm` 3.8.0), which tags each word and finds the grammatical links between words.

- **Words per sentence.** Mean sentence length (Lu 2010, L2 Syntactic Complexity Analyzer).
- **Subordinate clauses per sentence.** Finite dependent clauses: a "which", "that", "because", "when" or "if" clause with its own verb (Lu 2010).
- **Nominalizations per 100 words.** Nouns ending -tion, -ment, -ness or -ity, such as "representation" or "improvement" (Biber 1988).
- **Numbers per 100 words.** Numerals written in digits. Digits inside a name (QM9, ECFP4) do not count.
- **% sentences with 3+ numbers.** Share of sentences carrying three or more numerals.
- **Lexical density.** Nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs as a share of all words (Ure 1971). High means packed; low means many articles and prepositions.
- **Hedges and boosters per 100 words.** Hyland (2005) word lists. Hedges soften a claim (may, likely, suggests). Boosters harden it (clearly, must, in fact; "show/find that"). "About" and "around" count only before a number.
- **AI-guide phrases per 100 words.** The phrases in your `ai_phrasing.rtf` (e.g. "indicates that", "emerged as") plus style words that rose sharply in papers after ChatGPT (Kobak et al. 2025: delve, underscore, notably, crucial, comprehensive, insights, highlight, enhance, additionally). Field terms such as "robust" and "landscape" are left out.
- **% passive sentences** and **we/our/I per 100 words** (self-mention, Hyland 2005).
- **Moves.** What each sentence does, coded by reading (Swales 1990; Yang and Allison 2003): M1 method restated, M2 pointer to a figure or table, M3 result as numbers, M4 result in words, M5 comparison of results, M6 interpretation, M7 comparison with literature, M8 claim of strength, M9 limitation, M10 link, M11 recommendation. Moves exist only for results, discussion and conclusion sections. Your two versions were coded in full; each reference was coded on a sample of about 255 sentences (whole paragraphs, drawn per chapter and section type). Dablander was coded for this report (`codes_dablander.txt`); the others come from the earlier move files. One coder, one pass, no agreement check.
- **% M3 among M2+M3+M4.** Of the sentences that report a result or point to one, the share that gives it as numbers.

**Reference range.** The lowest and highest of the three theses on that measure. OUTSIDE means your value falls outside that range. The gap is the distance from the nearest end of the range, in standard deviations of the reference paragraphs of that section type. A gap of 1.0 means one typical paragraph-to-paragraph spread beyond the most extreme reference thesis. Gaps under 0.1 are negligible.

## 4. What the benchmark cannot see

- Whether a sentence is true, or a number is right. Every measure counts form, not content.
- Chemistry. A wrong chemical term scores the same as a right one.
- Order and flow across paragraphs. The moves count what each sentence does, not whether the next paragraph follows from the last. Repetition between the abstract, Chapter 1 and Chapter 8 is not measured.
- Figures, tables and captions. They were removed before counting, so a paragraph that points to a good table is not credited for it.
- Tense, spelling (American or British), capitalization of titles, and citation practice. None is measured.
- Whether the references are the right target. Three theses is a narrow range: two from protein-ligand and generative work, one on molecular featurization. Being OUTSIDE their range is a difference, not an error. Their results sections report fewer datasets than yours.
- Move coding is one reader, one pass, with no second coder. Your thesis was coded in full by earlier sessions; each reference on a sample of about 255 sentences. Dablander was coded in this run. Move shares from a sample of 40 to 190 sentences per section type move by several points with a different sample.
- The word lists count words, not senses. Hyland checks each hit by hand; this run did not. Clear false hits in your field were removed: "suspect label", "known flag", "true label", "certain datasets". Others remain, such as "should" in a method rule.
- The AI-phrase count is not an AI detector. The reference theses score higher on it than you do, mostly from "suggests that" and "indicates that".
- Whether a paragraph is AI-written. "AI-marked" only means a manual-review comment sits above it. A paragraph rewritten by AI with no marker counts as yours.
- Parsing errors. LaTeX residue, stripped maths ("where is the target standard deviation") and pdftotext residue (joined hyphens, maths fragments in Dablander) confuse the parser. Display equations split a paragraph in two, so some worklist paragraphs start mid-sentence.
- Paragraphs under 40 words. They are left out of the worklist.

# Slide-prep synthesis — what's actually worth showing across the 43 experiments

## 1. The results worth a slide (safe now)

These are trustworthy on the current numbers. Ranked by how much weight they can carry.

1. **The core claim holds, but only where the folds are big enough to trust it — and that itself is the honest headline.** In c02 (same model, best single rep vs. hybrid) the band-clearing wins fall into three clean clusters:
   - **Molecular ADME:** `adme_hlm` (svm on pdv) 0.454→0.421 error, +0.033 with a ±0.005 half-width, and `adme_mdr1` (svm on smited) +0.035 ±0.021. Tight and real.
   - **Genomic:** `nt_promoter_tata` (lightgbm on kmer5) 0.661→0.710, +0.049 ±0.008, and `gb_human_enhancers_cohn` (lightgbm on grover_dna) 0.480→0.506, +0.026 ±0.011. Two-for-two, both clear the band.
   - **Large-n antibody classification:** `tz_her2_binary` (lightgbm on blosum) 0.865→0.915, +0.051 ±0.041, and `tz_her2_multiclass` (xgboost on blosum) 0.712→0.766, +0.054 ±0.044 — both ~5000-sample sets.
   Plus three antibody regression properties that genuinely clear their bars: `flab_expression_koenig` (lightgbm on esm3) 0.681→0.772, +0.091 ±0.015 (the best antibody result), `flab_pk_jain_heprt` (ridge on prot_t5) +0.060 ±0.044, and `ddg_combined` (+0.108 ±0.079 at rank-2, +0.160 ±0.102 at rank-3). The defensible sentence for the deck is "hybrid clearly helps on molecular ADME, genomic promoter/enhancer, large-n tz_her2 classification, and a few tight-band antibody properties" — **not** "wins on 25 of 38." The 50–240-sample antibody wins (`abagym_EGFR` +0.034 against ±0.091, etc.) sit inside their error bars and would not survive an outer split.

2. **The motivating negative is clean and slide-ready: single reps barely track activity except on separable classification tasks.** In s01, distance-based spatial autocorrelation stays weak (SAC ~0.05–0.26, violation ~0.5) across every molecular, antibody, and reaction regression task, and only three group-separation tasks show real structure: `tcga_luad_vs_lusc` (wsi_titan, SAC 0.471, violation 0.113), `tcga_brca_pam50` (gene_geneformer, SAC 0.44–0.475, violation 0.079), and `humanness_therapeutic` (igt5, SAC 0.289, violation 0.133). This is the "why hybridize" slide — the split is classification-vs-regression, not molecular-vs-antibody.

3. **Solo-rep profiling gives a trustworthy rep-selection map on the large classification tasks.** c01 shows effective rank tracking score: `tcga_luad_vs_lusc` geneformer 0.949, `humanness_therapeutic` igt5 0.920 (ridge, robust), `tcga_brca_pam50` geneformer 0.882, `tz_her2_binary` onehot 0.787, `herg` mhg_gnn 0.773. Good as a supporting "which rep to reach for" panel; do not use its small-n antibody/Ginkgo rows (`ginkgo_aggregation` 0.164, `flab_aggregation_jain` all 23 reps at MI=0) as anything but a floor illustration.

## COMPLEXITY

## c01 — profiling each representation solo, no hybrid tested

**Interesting? For which datasets?** The first thing to be honest about is that c01 is not a hybrid experiment and produces no improvement number. It never builds a hybrid, so there is no "hybrid vs best existing" delta to slide anywhere. Every row is just: on this dataset, which single representation predicts best on its own, and how rich (effective rank) and informative (mutual information) that one winning representation is. So the question "which datasets improved" does not apply — nothing is being improved against anything. What the table gives you instead is a reference map of the strongest solo representation per dataset, and that map does have a real, non-noise pattern worth naming.

The genuinely strong and interpretable solo predictors all sit on the tasks that behave like classification, and they line up with high effective rank and real mutual information — the "rich, not compact" story holds where the data is large enough to see it: tcga_luad_vs_lusc (gene_geneformer, 0.949, erank 545, MI 0.63), tcga_brca_pam50 (gene_geneformer_v, 0.882, erank 487, MI 0.99), humanness_therapeutic (igt5, 0.920, erank 246 — the local file confirms this is ridge at 0.9125, not a fluke, since xgboost/svm/lightgbm all land 0.88-0.91), tz_her2_binary (onehot, 0.787), and herg (mhg_gnn, 0.773). Those are the rows a rep-selection reader would actually trust. On the flip side the developability antibody and Ginkgo tasks are where the profiling breaks down as a measurement: ginkgo_aggregation (0.164), ginkgo_tm2 (0.175), ginkgo_purity (0.242) barely clear zero even for their best rep, and a whole cluster — ginkgo_aggregation, ginkgo_purity, ginkgo_titer, flab_aggregation_jain, flab_immunogenicity_marks — reports mutual information of exactly 0.000. That zero is not "no relationship"; it is the estimator flooring at the label-permutation null because the sample is too small to detect anything, and on flab_aggregation_jain all 23 representations floor to zero. So the MI column is uninformative, not informative, on precisely the small antibody sets.

Two caveats keep this from being slide-grade even as a reference. The score column silently mixes metrics and directions with no unit: caco2's "pdv 0.293" is a lower-is-better error (a good result), while abagym_EGFR's "saprot 0.322" is a higher-is-better correlation, and the table gives no way to tell them apart. And every score is a hidden maximum over roughly six models with the winning model discarded — abagym_EGFR's saprot 0.322 comes only from the SVM, with its RF and MLP near zero. Net: this is a supporting reference table, not a headline. It earns a place only as context for which solo rep to reach for per domain, not as evidence the method does anything.

**Slide-safe now?** No, wait for rerun. Not because the numbers are degenerate in the c15/c21 sense, but because the local result files are stale relative to the report and disagree on the exact column that would be quoted: the report prints saprot MI 0.858 for abagym_EGFR and igt5 MI 1.442 for humanness_therapeutic, while the local files for the same dataset/rep show MI 0.000, and the molecular rows (caco2, herg, adme_*) have no local file at all. The report was generated from a newer server run I cannot see, so the MI figures specifically are unverifiable from disk. If anything from c01 goes on a slide it should be the qualitative rep-strength ranking (geneformer wins the TCGA tasks, igt5 wins humanness, onehot/blosum win the tz_her2 antibody tasks), not the raw MI numbers. The honest post-rerun version of this table should carry a metric-name and direction label per row and mark the floored-at-null zeros as "below detection," at which point the strong-classification rows become citable and the near-zero Ginkgo rows should be read as "not measurable at this n" rather than results.

**Figure ideas.** (1) A per-dataset horizontal bar of the best solo rep's score, split into two panels by metric direction so the lower-is-better molecular error bars are never placed next to the higher-is-better antibody/tcga correlation bars, each bar annotated with the winning rep name — this shows the rep-selection map without the misleading cross-metric comparison. (2) A scatter of best-rep score (y) against that rep's effective rank (x), one dot per dataset, colored by domain, to test the "rich beats compact" claim visually; the tcga/humanness/tz_her2 points in the high-score high-rank corner are the story, and the Ginkgo cluster sitting low would show the failure honestly. (3) Skip any figure built on the MI column until the rerun labels the floored zeros — as it stands MI would draw a column of fake zeros across the antibody sets.


## c02 — the core claim: same model, best single rep vs. hybrid

**Interesting? For which datasets?** This is the project's headline test, and the honest reading is that it works clearly on exactly the datasets where the numbers are trustworthy, and is mostly within noise where they are not. The trustworthy wins split into three clean clusters. Molecular is a clean sweep of four with tight bands: adme_hlm (svm on pdv, 0.454→0.421 error, +0.033, half-width only 0.005, band-clearing WIN*) and adme_mdr1 (svm on smited, +0.035, ±0.021, WIN*) are the two solid ones; herg_ki (+0.028, ±0.048) and caco2 (+0.024, ±0.024) win but do not clear their bands. Genomic is two-for-two and both clear the band: nt_promoter_tata (lightgbm on kmer5, 0.661→0.710, +0.049, ±0.008) and gb_human_enhancers_cohn (lightgbm on grover_dna, 0.480→0.506, +0.026, ±0.011). Large-n antibody classification is the third solid cluster: tz_her2_binary (lightgbm on blosum, 0.865→0.915, +0.051, ±0.041, WIN*) and tz_her2_multiclass (xgboost on blosum, 0.712→0.766, +0.054, ±0.044, WIN*), each a ~5000-sample set with a believable gain.

Inside the 30-dataset antibody/protein block the direction is positive (the bottom tally reads +19 / =5 / -6), but most of those wins sit deep inside their own error bars — abagym_EGFR is the archetype: +0.034 improvement against a ±0.091 half-width, so the "WIN" label is not statistically distinguishable from fold noise. The antibody datasets that genuinely stand out because they clear the band are worth naming individually: flab_expression_koenig (lightgbm on esm3, 0.681→0.772, +0.091, ±0.015 — a large, tight, real gain and the best antibody result here), flab_pk_jain_heprt (ridge on prot_t5, 0.735→0.795, +0.060, ±0.044), and ddg_combined (rank-1 ridge is +0.107 but inside its 0.153 band; the rank-2 lightgbm/prot_t5 +0.108 ±0.079 and rank-3 rf/prot_t5_cdr +0.160 ±0.102 are both band-clearing). Everything else in the antibody block — the flab_thermo_jain +0.101 (±0.180), abagym_Ang2 +0.068 (±0.401), ginkgo_tm2 +0.101 (±0.190) type rows — is a real positive tilt but not a per-dataset claim you can defend. The genuine losses to name are abagym_HER2 (−0.092), ginkgo_purity (−0.055 on the deployed model), abcov_kd (−0.060), and flab_aggregation_jain (−0.041). TCGA is a correct non-result: tcga_luad_vs_lusc (0.991) and tcga_brca_pam50 (0.874) are already at the ceiling and land as ties, as does humanness_therapeutic (0.969), so the hybrid rightly has nothing to add.

One structural caveat that matters for the antibody deltas specifically: the hybrid is the argmax over ~4,445 configurations on the same folds it is scored on, while the baseline is the best of a few dozen reps, so a search over thousands of noisy small-n fold-means will surface lucky highs. On the 2000–5000-sample molecular/genomic/classification sets that bias is small; on the 50–240-sample antibody sets it is a real part of the +0.03-inside-±0.09 wins. So c02 deserves importance, but the defensible headline is "hybrid clearly helps on molecular ADME, genomic promoter/enhancer, and large-n tz_her2 classification, plus a few tight-band antibody properties, and is within noise on the small antibody sets" — not "wins on 25 of 38."

**Slide-safe now?** Partial. c02 is not on the degenerate/stale list, so its numbers are honest. Slide-safe as individual claims: adme_hlm, adme_mdr1 (molecular, band-clearing); nt_promoter_tata, gb_human_enhancers_cohn (genomic, band-clearing); tz_her2_binary, tz_her2_multiclass (large-n classification, band-clearing); flab_expression_koenig, flab_pk_jain_heprt, ddg_combined (antibody, band-clearing at rank 1 or 2/3). Do NOT put the small-n antibody "WIN" rows (abagym_*, most flab_*, ginkgo_*) on a slide as individual wins — they are inside their confidence bands and would not survive an outer split. One missing cell to flag: ginkgo_aggregation's rank-1 svm hybrid produced no finite score (blank row), so its deployed-model verdict is empty.

## c04 — importance scores reused as a distance metric (weighted vs plain kNN)

**Interesting? For which datasets?** This is a methodology probe, not a hybrid-vs-baseline result, and its honest signal is much thinner than the "+31 helps, -0 hurts" tally suggests. The one finding worth stating is a *conditional*: importance-weighting a concatenated feature space helps a nearest-neighbour predictor mostly when plain Euclidean kNN is already broken by dimensionality, and it does little or nothing when the plain baseline is healthy. The raw files make this clean. The biggest reported deltas all sit on top of a negative unweighted R2 — i.e. a baseline worse than predicting the mean: abagym_lysozyme goes from unweighted R2 -0.40 to weighted +0.07 (the "+0.47"); ddg_combined from -0.79 to -0.51 (still useless, just less useless, yet printed as "+0.28"); flab_pk_jain_heprt from +0.07 to +0.41; flab_aggregation_jain_hicrt from -0.07 to +0.14. These are the curse of dimensionality being partially repaired, not evidence that importance is a good similarity metric.

The genuinely informative rows are the ones where the *unweighted* baseline already works. buchwald_hartwig (the only reaction dataset here) has a healthy plain-kNN R2 of 0.62 and weighting nudges it to 0.66 — a real but modest +0.04, all three importance methods agree. flab_thermo_tresanco goes 0.35 to 0.42 (+0.07, all three agree). And the telling counter-example: flab_immunogenicity_marks has a decent baseline (R2 0.17) and all three importance methods *hurt* it at k=10, yet the table still prints deeplift at "+0.019" because the reader shows the best-of-three method. That single cherry-pick is the whole problem with the headline.

On the spread across methods: the "-0 hurts" claim is an artifact of taking a max over three methods per dataset. Reading the raw JSON, abagym_VEGF's printed "+0.009" (deeplift, essentially a tie) hides lightgbm_gain at -0.23 and treeshap at -0.19 on the same data; abagym_HER2 has 2 of 3 methods hurting; flab_aggregation_jain 2 of 3; flab_immunogenicity_marks 3 of 3. So the true per-dataset story is "sometimes helps, often method-dependent, occasionally uniformly hurts," not a clean sweep.

No domain cluster carries this. The large deltas are scattered across antibody developability (abagym, flab, ginkgo) and the ddg protein set, and they track *baseline brokenness*, not domain. Molecular rows (caco2 +0.06, adme_hlm +0.15, herg_ki +0.065) are unremarkable. All classification datasets (tz_her2_binary/multiclass, humanness_therapeutic, tcga_luad_vs_lusc, nt_promoter_tata, gb_*) are honestly skipped because the retrieval predictor averages continuous labels. Bottom line: this experiment deserves low importance as a results slide; its real value is the conditional methodology point, and even that needs the reader rewritten to stop cherry-picking the best method.

**Slide-safe now?** Partial. The individual raw R2 pairs I cite above are trustworthy and safe to quote. What is NOT slide-safe is the reader's "importance-weighted kNN helps: +31, hurts: -0" tally and the per-dataset delta column — both are a maximum over three importance methods on top of frequently-broken baselines, so they overstate the effect. c04 is not on the degenerate/stale rerun list, so the underlying numbers will not change, but the *presentation* must not be used as-is. Also note the cross_test transfer matrix and the low-dim (budget-25) analysis are computed and saved in every JSON but never reach the report, so the actual "is importance a transferable metric" question is currently untold.

**Figure ideas.**
1. Diverging dot plot, one row per regression dataset, x-axis = kNN R2. Plot the unweighted R2 and the best weighted R2 as two marks joined by a line, sorted by unweighted R2. This makes the real story visible at a glance: the long lines (big deltas) all start from negative unweighted R2, while healthy baselines (buchwald_hartwig, flab_thermo_tresanco) barely move.
2. Small-multiple of per-method deltas: for each dataset show three dots (deeplift, lightgbm_gain, treeshap) at their k=10 delta, colour-coded, with a zero line. This exposes the method disagreement the tally hides — VEGF, HER2, immunogenicity_marks will show dots straddling or below zero.
3. Scatter: x = unweighted kNN R2, y = best weighted delta, one point per dataset. Expect a strong negative slope, which is the honest headline — weighting helps exactly where the plain metric is failing. This one figure replaces the misleading "+31" line.


## c05 — learning curves: does the hybrid's edge grow or shrink as n grows?

## c06 — within-family capacity gradient (more model capacity → larger optimal budget?)

**Interesting? For which datasets?** Essentially nothing, and this is a design problem, not bad luck. The experiment asks whether higher-capacity versions of the same model type want more features, and it answers with a Spearman rho computed over exactly four capacity rungs per family. A four-point correlation can only take a handful of discrete values and swings to ±0.77 or ±1.0 on pure chance, so there is no dataset here where the number means anything. The p-values in the JSONs make this undeniable: the only "perfect" correlations are the degenerate ones. On buchwald_hartwig, XGBoost gives rho = -1.0 with p = 0.0 — but that is four monotone points saying more capacity wanted *fewer* features (budgets 2000, 1000, 500, 50), the exact opposite of the hypothesis, while on the same dataset SVM gives rho = +0.77 (p = 0.23) saying the opposite again. abagym_EGFR is the same story: MLP rho = -0.26 (p = 0.74) and XGBoost rho = -0.26 (p = 0.74), both statistically meaningless. Every non-degenerate p-value in the local data sits around 0.22 to 0.74. So the wide rho ranges the report prints (abagym_EGFR "[-0.775,+0.738]", buchwald_hartwig "[-0.894,+1.000]") are not five families disagreeing about a real effect — they are five independent draws of four-point noise.

The many "0 measured" rows — flab_aggregation_jain, flab_pk_jain_fcrnrt, ginkgo_acsins, ginkgo_colloidal, ginkgo_hic, ginkgo_titer — are the small antibody-developability panels (94–242 samples). There every capacity rung picks the same single budget (almost always 50), so the correlation is honestly undefined and the experiment reports nothing rather than a fake number. That is the correct behaviour, but it also means the datasets that *do* print a range are just the ones where noise happened to flip one budget. No domain or assay cluster carries a signal; the antibody sets, the one reaction set, and the rest all behave identically as noise or as "collapsed to one budget." There is no dataset worth a slide here.

One structural note the prior analysis already established: the local JSONs are stale (older schema, budgets only in {50, 500} for many rows, no "status" field), so the exact rho values in the report come from newer server runs I cannot open. But the mechanism is schema-independent — a rho over four rungs is noise regardless of which run produced it, and I verified the four-point shape and the non-significant p-values directly from the local files.

**Slide-safe now?** no-wait-for-rerun — and, bluntly, probably not slide-safe even after the rerun. The local files are stale so the current numbers are not authoritative, but the deeper issue is that a clean rerun would still compute a Spearman correlation over four capacity rungs, which cannot be significant. The honest version of this experiment only becomes meaningful if the capacity ladders are given many more rungs (so the correlation has something to bite on) or if it stops reporting a per-family rho entirely and instead shows the budget-versus-capacity curve for a human to read. As currently designed, the rerun will measure the same four-point noise with correct-but-stale-free arithmetic.

**Figure ideas.** Nothing here is figure-worthy as a result. The only defensible figure is a diagnostic that *demonstrates the experiment is underpowered*: a small-multiple of budget-versus-capacity-rung line plots (x = capacity index 1–4, y = winning budget) for the handful of families that printed a non-NaN rho — buchwald_hartwig XGBoost, SVM, RF, MLP side by side — so a viewer can see the four points jump around with no trend. If you want a single summary mark, a strip of the per-family p-values across all datasets would show them clustered near 0.2–0.7 and nowhere near significance, which is the real takeaway. Do not make an averaged rho bar; it would launder noise into an apparent effect.

## c08 — does the winning hybrid produce honestly calibrated uncertainty?

**Interesting? For which datasets?** This is a calibration diagnostic, not a hybrid-lift experiment: there is no "hybrid vs best existing rep" delta anywhere in it. ## s01 — do single-rep distances track activity? (the motivating "no")

**Interesting? ## n13 — does the hybrid's edge survive changing the shape of the injected noise

**Interesting? For which datasets?** This is a small sensitivity probe, not a robustness benchmark, and in its current form it is close to nothing you can put weight on. At that resolution there is not much to separate signal from noise.

# Rep dominance — when does the hybrid actually help? (added 2026-07-08, local c01 + c02)

The honest boundary: the hybrid helps when NO single representation dominates the
task, and is redundant (or slightly hurts, within the small-n noise) when one
representation already carries the signal. From the per-rep leaderboard (c01, best
score across models per rep) and the hybrid lift (c02, best hybrid vs best single).

**One rep dominates → hybrid can't add (the honest-limit cases):**
- `abagym_HER2`: mutation_features 0.453, next rep ~0.18 behind — a landslide. Hybrid
  lift −0.068. The mutations ARE the signal for this affinity-maturation DMS;
  embeddings are noise.
- `flab_pk_jain_fcrnrt`: ablang2 0.595 clearly ahead; hybrid −0.081.
- `flab_polyreact_jain_cic`: prot_t5 leads; hybrid −0.011 (flat).
- `ginkgo_hic`: esm2_concat 0.582 is essentially enough — hybrid lift only +0.008.
  (Flagged elsewhere as a "headline developability" candidate, but the data says one
  rep suffices — NOT a hybrid story.)
- `ginkgo_colloidal`, `ginkgo_pr_cho`: prot_t5 enough, lift ~0.

**No single rep dominates → hybrid helps:**
- `abagym_EGFR`: top reps within ~0.06 of each other (no leader); hybrid +0.083.
- `ddg_combined`: esm2_wt_delta leads modestly but hybrid still +0.107 — combining
  adds real signal on top of a decent rep.
- `flab_polyreact_jain_bvp` +0.075, `flab_thermo_jain` +0.073,
  `flab_pk_jain_heprt` +0.056, `ginkgo_heparin` +0.043.

**The killer contrast slide:** `abagym_EGFR` vs `abagym_HER2` — SAME single-antibody
DMS setup, opposite outcome. On EGFR no rep dominates and the hybrid gains +0.083; on
HER2 mutation_features is a landslide and the hybrid adds nothing. Rep-dominance
predicts whether KIRBy helps, holding the assay fixed.

Caveat: antibody lifts have wide 5-fold CIs (small n), so individual +/- values are
not all significant — but the DOMINANCE (from c01) is robust, and the molecular /
genomic wins (adme_*, nt_*, gb_*) are the significant c02 cases. Confirm on re-run.

## Per-experiment analysis

### c01_rep_characterization — PUBLISH (methodology)

**What it does:** Per-rep intrinsic dimensionality (nominal/PCA-95/MLE), mutual information with target, effective rank, and 6-model baseline scores.

**Coverage:** 12/13 (Ang2 missing — re-running).

**Findings:**
- 20-27 reps per dataset; nominal dim 28-17920.
- Best baselines vary 0.359 (polyreact_bvp) to 0.914 (humanness AUROC) — domain-aware metric is firing correctly (R²/ρ for regression, AUROC for classification).
- **Winning reps are dataset-dependent, not universal:** `igt5` wins flab_pk_jain_heprt (ρ=0.782), `mutation_features` wins abagym_HER2 (R²=0.453), `aac` wins abagym_VEGF (R²=0.634), `ablang2`/`antiberty` win polyreact. PLMs are strongest in general but domain-specific reps win on data-poor / mutation-driven datasets.

**Verdict:** **PUBLISH** as the rep-characterization table. Foundational figure for the paper.

### c02_complexity_matching_grid — PUBLISH (headline KIRBy result)

**What it does:** Compares best hybrid (per condition: `no_aug`, `uncapped`, `capped`, `top_tier_only`, `frozen_plus_ft`, `per_model_top_rep_dim`) against best single-rep baseline and concat→PCA baselines.

**Coverage:** 12/13.

**Findings (Δ = best hybrid − best baseline):**

| Dataset | Baseline | Best Hybrid | Δ | Best condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| abagym_Ang2 | 0.474 | 0.609 | **+0.135** | top_tier_only |
| flab_polyreact_jain_bvp | 0.374 | 0.458 | **+0.084** | top_tier_only |
| flab_aggregation_jain | 0.425 | 0.492 | **+0.067** | top_tier_only (aug) |
| flab_pk_jain_heprt | 0.782 | 0.848 | **+0.066** | capped (aug) |
| ginkgo_heparin | 0.711 | 0.768 | **+0.057** | capped (aug) |
| abagym_EGFR | 0.324 | 0.374 | **+0.050** | frozen_plus_ft |
| flab_immunogenicity_marks | 0.399 | 0.421 | **+0.022** | top_tier_only |
| abcov_kd | 0.460 | 0.474 | **+0.014** | top_tier_only |
| abagym_VEGF | 0.633 | 0.638 | +0.005 | top_tier_only |
| humanness_therapeutic | 0.966 | 0.970 | +0.004 | top_tier_only |
| flab_polyreact_jain_cic | 0.585 | 0.581 | -0.004 | per_model |
| abagym_HER2 | 0.453 | 0.433 | -0.020 | top_tier_only |

- **10/12 datasets: hybrid ≥ baseline.** Wins on 8/12 by Δ > 0.01.
- **PCA-concat baseline never wins** — universally below both hybrid and single-rep baseline across all 12 datasets (range 0.135–0.914, always < best hybrid).
- **`top_tier_only` is the strongest condition** for non-augmented datasets; **`capped` wins for all 3 augmented datasets**.
- The two losses (HER2, polyreact_cic) are both small and on datasets where `best_single` is already very strong (low-dim domain features `mutation_features` and `prot_t5`).

**Verdict:** **PUBLISH.** Headline KIRBy figure. Note the failure modes honestly (HER2 mutation_features case is a known limitation — hybrid doesn't help when one specialized low-dim rep dominates).

### c04_importance_as_metric — METHODOLOGICAL

**What it does:** Tests whether feature importance scores (deeplift/lightgbm_gain/treeshap) generalize across importance methods and whether weighting kNN/similarity by importance helps.

**Coverage:** 12/13.

**Findings (abagym_HER2 example):**
- knn(5) deeplift weighting: Δ R² = +0.011 (slight improvement)
- knn(20) deeplift weighting: Δ R² = -0.020 (hurts)
- similarity weighting Δ MAE: +0.003 (negligible)
- Cross-method R² (deeplift score evaluated on lightgbm_gain-selected features etc.) shows importance scores are mostly method-specific.

**Verdict:** **METHODOLOGICAL.** Justifies the choice of importance method in the pipeline; informs reviewers that we picked an importance method deliberately. Not a standalone result.

### c11_ensemble_vs_hybrid — PUBLISH-WITH-CAVEATS (negative result has value)

**What it does (post-patch):** Compares 3 methods at matched dimensions: `best_single`, `pca_matched` (concat→PCA→model), `hybrid` (KIRBy selection + model).

**Coverage:** 9/13.

**Findings:**

| Dataset | best_single | pca_matched | hybrid | hybrid > single? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| abagym_Ang2 | 0.380 | 0.389 | **0.497** | ✓ (+0.117) |
| abagym_EGFR | 0.322 | 0.318 | 0.338 | ✓ (+0.015) |
| abagym_HER2 | **0.453** | 0.134 | 0.231 | ✗ (-0.222) |
| abagym_VEGF | **0.634** | 0.439 | 0.633 | flat |
| abcov_kd | **0.484** | 0.355 | 0.391 | ✗ (-0.093) |
| flab_immunogenicity_marks | 0.359 | 0.312 | 0.362 | ✓ (+0.003) |
| flab_polyreact_jain_bvp | 0.374 | 0.338 | 0.396 | ✓ (+0.022) |
| flab_polyreact_jain_cic | **0.585** | 0.478 | 0.527 | ✗ (-0.058) |
| humanness_therapeutic | 0.914 | 0.914 | 0.922 | ✓ (+0.007) |

- 5/9 hybrid wins, 4/9 loses. Median Δ ≈ +0.01.
- `pca_matched` is universally weaker than `hybrid` and `best_single` — confirms concat→PCA is the wrong baseline (which we already knew).
- The 4 losses are explained by best_single being a low-dim domain-specific rep (`mutation_features` for HER2, AAC-family) that already saturates the signal at very small dim.

**Verdict:** **PUBLISH-WITH-CAVEATS** as a *fairness check*. c02 already shows hybrid winning at unmatched dimensions; c11 is the strict matched-dim version. Frame as: "hybrid wins when no single rep dominates; when one specialized low-dim rep is sufficient, hybrid is not needed." This is honest and a stronger paper than pretending hybrid always wins.

### c13_forward_compatibility — PUBLISH (with framing)

**What it does:** When new reps (esm3, esmc, saprot, etc.) are added, does the hybrid improve? Reports `win_rate_D_vs_C` (new condition vs existing-only) and `win_rate_E_vs_B`.

**Coverage:** 7/13.

**Findings:**

| Dataset | D_vs_C | E_vs_B | n_new_reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| abagym_Ang2 | 0.53 | 0.67 | 15 |
| abagym_HER2 | 0.29 | 0.43 | 7 |
| abagym_VEGF | 0.47 | 0.53 | 15 |
| abcov_kd | 0.33 | 0.33 | 15 |
| flab_immunogenicity_marks | 0.20 | 0.60 | 15 |
| flab_polyreact_jain_bvp | 0.92 | 0.58 | 12 |
| flab_polyreact_jain_cic | 0.43 | 0.79 | 14 |

- D_vs_C median ≈ 0.43; E_vs_B median ≈ 0.58.
- Mean E_vs_B ≈ 0.56 — modest but consistent advantage when adding new reps.
- D_vs_C is noisier; sometimes negative (immuno 0.20, abcov 0.33).

**Verdict:** **PUBLISH** but frame as "forward compatibility on average, not universal." The thesis ("adding new reps via KIRBy generally improves") is supported by E_vs_B but D_vs_C is mixed. Publish the E_vs_B view as headline, D_vs_C as supplement.

### c16_feature_selection_as_regularization — PUBLISH (methodology)

**What it does (post-patch):** Per-family (Ridge/RF/SVM) heatmap of `(val_R², train_R², gap)` across budgets × regularization hyperparam. Tests whether feature selection substitutes for regularization.

**Coverage:** 7/13.

**Findings (abagym_Ang2 Ridge):**
- Budget 25, alpha 0.001: val 0.370, train 0.810, gap 0.441
- Budget 25, alpha 100: val 0.431, train 0.809, gap 0.424
- → at very small budget, increasing alpha helps; gap shrinks slowly. Suggests low-budget hybrid doesn't fully replace regularization.

**Verdict:** **PUBLISH** as a methodology section figure. Cleaner story than expected and addresses the inevitable "isn't this just regularization?" reviewer question.

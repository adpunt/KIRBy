  The real problem being modelled

  An assay has a detection limit. If a compound is more potent than the instrument can read, the record doesn't say how potent — it says the limit. So a block of molecules that genuinely differ all get written down with the same
  number. Svensson et al. found 25–63% of records censored this way in ten of fifteen industrial assays.

  What I did to herg_ki to create it

  Took the 941 training molecules. Found the pKi value that the top 10% sit above — on fold 0 that was 6.86 pKi. Set all 94 of those molecules' labels to exactly 6.86. Their true values ranged from 6.86 upwards; now they all read
  6.86. Held-out molecules were left alone, so the test set is honest.

  The correction

  Those 94 molecules are findable without any secret knowledge — they are the ones whose label is exactly the maximum. Ninety-five molecules share the value 6.86; with no clipping, only one molecule sits at the maximum. That
  pile-up is the giveaway.

  weren't clipped, then the mean of everything falling above a cut-off sits a computable distance above that cut-off — the mean of a normal distribution truncated at that point.
